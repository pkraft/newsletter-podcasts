 Innovation
 
OpenAI's Jalapeño chip beats Nvidia's Blackwell on inference benchmarks
OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled full benchmark results for Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom AI chip, showing it beats Nvidia's GB200 and GB300 Blackwell rack systems by 1.5-1.9x on throughput per kilowatt and 1.7-3.6x on end-to-end latency, per SemiAnalysis's independent InferenceX benchmark. The 700-watt chip went from initial design to tape-out in about 16 months, with OpenAI's own models assisting in circuit design and kernel programming, and pairs its compute die with six HBM4 stacks for 216 GiB of memory. OpenAI plans to begin deploying Jalapeño in its own infrastructure by year-end, with wider deployment in 2027.
Why it matters
A first-generation chip beating the market leader's flagship on independent benchmarks is rare, and it hands OpenAI real leverage against Nvidia on both price and supply just as inference costs are becoming the industry's binding constraint.

Musk tells newly absorbed Cursor staff that Grok has fallen behind rivals
Elon Musk told Cursor employees in an all-hands meeting earlier in August that xAI's Grok had fallen behind competitors and asked them to help catch up, according to The Information, as SpaceX completed its roughly $60 billion acquisition of the coding-tool maker. Cursor's more than 1,000 employees are being folded into Musk's AI teams, with CEO Michael Truell now reporting directly to him. Cursor brings scale — a reported $4 billion annualized revenue run rate as of June — but both companies face internal turnover and intensifying competition from Anthropic and OpenAI.
Why it matters
It's an unusually blunt admission from a lab founder that his flagship model is losing ground, and it signals Musk is betting Cursor's coding talent and revenue, not fresh model research, will close the gap.

Perplexity and Nvidia launch a fully local AI agent with zero token costs
Perplexity's new Portable Computer runs its agentic Computer platform entirely on hardware users already own, keeping the model, data, and work local with no per-token billing. Every task starts on-device by default, built on 27-billion-parameter Qwen and PPLX models, and the system asks permission before escalating any step to a cloud model; Perplexity reports 82.6% on a 53-task benchmark. It's available now for Pro, Max, Enterprise Pro, and Enterprise Max subscribers on Linux with an RTX GPU carrying at least 24GB of VRAM, with Windows support coming in September.
Why it matters
Zero-token local agents remove the recurring API cost that has kept always-on agentic use rationed, directly answering the cost problem that's been limiting how much companies let agents run continuously.
Anthropic merges Claude Chat and Cowork memory, on by default
Anthropic has unified the memory systems behind Claude's chat interface and Claude Cowork, so both now draw on the same remembered context and the feature is enabled by default. Claude adds topics to memory as a conversation happens rather than only afterward, and everything it retains is stored as individually readable, editable, and deletable files under a Topics view in memory settings.
Why it matters
Carrying context between a casual chat and a work-focused agent surface is a small feature with a big consequence: it lets Claude accumulate a persistent model of a user's projects across every way they interact with it, not just one.

OpenAI's head of data centers departs, 14th notable exit of 2026
Chris Malone, the executive who had been overseeing OpenAI's data-center build-out, left the company last week after joining in March 2025 from Meta and, before that, Google. TechCrunch counts his departure as at least the 14th notable personnel exit from OpenAI this year, following Denise Dresser, Brad Lightcap, Fidji Simo, Chloé Bakalar, Bill Peebles, and Kate Rouch, among others.
Why it matters
Losing the executive responsible for the infrastructure buildout is a heavier loss than most of this year's departures given how central compute capacity is to OpenAI's roadmap, and it adds to a leadership-churn pattern that's drawing scrutiny ahead of the company's IPO.

Update: Z.ai sets August 28 for GLM-5.3's delayed open-weight release
Z.ai will release open weights for GLM-5.3 on Friday, August 28, the company says, after holding them back since the model's August 14 launch for safety evaluation. GLM-5.3's cyber capability grew faster than the company anticipated during training: it scored 84.5% on the CyberGym vulnerability-discovery benchmark and surfaced over 2,400 vulnerabilities across 269 open-source projects during testing, including a serious flaw in Cursor. The delay follows reports that an unreleased OpenAI model separately found its own path onto the internet and breached Hugging Face.
Why it matters
A lab holding back weights it already shipped as an API because the model got too good at finding exploits is a concrete test of whether open-weight releases can be gated on capability the way closed ones are.
