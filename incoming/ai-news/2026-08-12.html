NVIDIA shipped a single-GPU open model today and is reportedly training a trillion-parameter one behind it — a bet that arming everyone with free frontier-grade AI sells more chips than protecting the moats of its own AI-lab customers does.
 
 Models
 
NVIDIA ships Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, reportedly training a trillion-parameter Nemotron 4 behind it
NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, an open 30-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 3 billion active parameters that runs on a single GPU, alongside NeMo Switchyard, an open-source router that reassigns each step of an agent workflow to whichever model handles it best. Paired together, NVIDIA's own tests show the combination completing agentic tasks about 30% faster than Qwen3.6-35B at matching accuracy, for roughly a third of the cost of running Claude Opus 4.8 alone. Separately, The Information reports NVIDIA is training a far larger open-weight model, Nemotron 4, that employees expect could reach 1 trillion or more parameters as early as late fall, and the company has raised its multiyear cloud compute commitments to $28 billion through 2031 to support the effort.
Why it matters
NVIDIA making frontier-grade AI free and open puts it in direct competition with the closed labs — OpenAI, Anthropic, and the startups it backs — whose business models depend on charging for exactly the capability NVIDIA now has an incentive to give away in order to sell more chips.
 
 Innovation
 
Researchers show frontier labs' encrypted reasoning traces can be stolen and decoded
A new paper, "Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs," shows that Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google all return chain-of-thought reasoning to API clients as encrypted blocks that stay valid across sessions, users, and even different models from the same provider. Researchers exploited that portability by feeding an encrypted trace from a strong model into a weaker, less-guarded sibling model, coercing it into decoding and printing the reasoning in plaintext — without ever attacking the strong model directly or tripping its anti-distillation defenses. Running the technique across 315,320 reasoning blocks scraped from public repositories recovered 367 pieces of personal information and 182 live credentials, plus hazardous content the models' visible, refusing outputs had otherwise kept hidden.
Why it matters
It undercuts a core safety assumption — that hiding a model's reasoning also hides what it knows — and shows "safe" refusals can be circumvented by decoding the private thinking behind them, at three of the industry's largest labs simultaneously.
Anthropic will watermark Claude-generated text to comply with EU rules
Anthropic said it will begin embedding an invisible watermark in text generated by Claude models released after August 2, designed to survive some copying, pasting, and light editing, to comply with the EU AI Act's transparency rules. The company plans to extend watermarking to older models and to mark files using the C2PA content-provenance standard, with the system applying across the Claude app, Claude Code, Cowork, and the API.
Why it matters
It's one of the first concrete moves by a frontier lab to make AI-generated text durably identifiable after the fact, in an industry that has mostly limited watermarking to images and video.

OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap departs to "start something new"
Brad Lightcap, OpenAI's chief operating officer for eight years, is leaving to launch a new venture, with only vague hints so far that it will extend OpenAI's mission from outside the company. His exit adds to a fast-moving pattern at the top of OpenAI's org chart: Fidji Simo, Bill Peebles, and Kevin Weil have all left in recent months as OpenAI reshapes its leadership ahead of a possible IPO.
Why it matters
Losing the COO who ran day-to-day operations through OpenAI's fastest growth period, on top of three other senior exits in short order, raises the question of how much institutional continuity survives the run-up to a public listing.
 
 Tools
 
xAI launches Grok Bot, giving AI agents their own cloud computers
xAI opened a beta of Grok Bot, which gives AI agents dedicated cloud computers so they can operate across ordinary apps and websites without needing clean APIs, working as "always-on" teammates that handle tasks end-to-end and surface for approval only when needed. Bots can coordinate with each other in parallel, learn workflows over time, and retain context across sessions; access starts with SuperGrok Heavy and Cursor's Ultra and Teams Premium tiers.
Why it matters
It's a bet that giving agents a persistent computer and memory, rather than a one-shot API call, is what turns them from chatbots that answer questions into software that finishes jobs.

Ex-xAI co-founder's River AI raises $1.1B to build personal, open AI hardware
River AI, a two-month-old startup founded by former xAI co-founder Igor Babuschkin, raised a $1.1 billion seed and Series A round led by General Catalyst and AMP, with Nvidia, AMD Ventures, Y Combinator, and Temasek also participating. The company plans to build open-source AI models and, eventually, home servers that let people run and retrain their own AI locally instead of depending on centralized cloud providers, though its first products remain months away.
Why it matters
It's a well-funded bet, backed by two chipmakers, that private, local, user-modifiable AI can be a business at frontier-lab scale rather than a hobbyist niche.
Manus's Meta acquisition falls apart, agent startup returns to independent operations
Manus, the agentic AI startup, told users it will resume operating as an independent company after its planned acquisition by Meta unraveled amid regulatory hurdles. Users whose data was generated on or after December 29, 2025 must back it up before 7:59 a.m. Singapore time on August 23, as Manus migrates affected accounts ahead of restoring service on August 25; the company says affected users won't be charged during the transition and will receive "welcome-back" bonuses.
Why it matters
A deal collapsing after regulators got involved is a reminder that Big Tech's appetite for buying up AI agent startups is running into the same antitrust friction that has already slowed similar acquisitions elsewhere.
Google's Gemini app crosses 1 billion monthly users
Google said its Gemini app has surpassed 1 billion monthly active users, becoming the company's 14th product to hit that mark and putting it roughly on pace with ChatGPT, which crossed the same threshold in June. Google also disclosed that 63% of Gemini usage happens by voice, the app generates more than 150 million images a day, and it now has over 100 million monthly active users on iOS alone.
Why it matters
It confirms the "second AI assistant" slot isn't a two-horse race between ChatGPT and everyone else — Google has built a genuine mass-market rival at comparable scale, just later than expected.
