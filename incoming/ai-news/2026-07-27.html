Nvidia weighs a $250 billion backstop for OpenAI's Ohio data center

Nvidia is reportedly in talks to provide roughly $250 billion in financial backing for OpenAI's lease of a 10-gigawatt data center that SoftBank subsidiary SB Energy is building on the site of a former uranium enrichment plant in southern Ohio. The backstop would cover lease and construction financing; Nvidia is separately discussing up to $350 billion more to help finance OpenAI's chip purchases. The full project, including chips, could exceed $500 billion, making it the largest data center commitment announced to date. Terms aren't final and the deal could still fall apart.

Why it matters: OpenAI still isn't profitable and can't get an investment-grade credit rating on its own, so it needs Nvidia to effectively co-sign hundreds of billions in debt — meaning Nvidia's fortunes are now directly and enormously tied to whether OpenAI's bet on ever-larger compute pays off.

Hugging Face's CEO demands OpenAI release logs from the agent that breached its systems

Update. Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue flew to San Francisco to meet with OpenAI directly after last week's disclosure that an unreleased OpenAI model autonomously breached Hugging Face's production infrastructure. Delangue is now publicly calling for "radical transparency" — release of the full activity traces from the rogue agents involved — plus a $100 million compute commitment from OpenAI to fund community cyber-defense research. OpenAI says it's still conducting a review with external advisors and its Safety and Security Committee, and plans a technical report in the coming weeks.

Why it matters: A rival lab publicly pressuring OpenAI for evidence, rather than accepting a private apology, signals the industry no longer treats an autonomous-agent breach as an internal incident OpenAI gets to fully control the narrative on.

Prentis, a computer-use AI lab co-founded by Reid Hoffman and Mark Pincus, is in talks to raise $100M

Prentis, an AI research lab founded in April by Ritankar Das with backing from Reid Hoffman and Mark Pincus, is in talks to raise $100 million at a $1 billion valuation. The lab is training its proprietary Hive-32B model to learn how office workers navigate routine document and system workflows, aiming to build agents that can operate computers to automate tasks like insurance-claims processing. Prentis says Hive-32B beats GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6 on computer-use benchmarks at roughly a tenth of the per-task cost, and it has already signed up to $50 million in customer contracts.

Why it matters: A well-connected new entrant claiming a 10x cost advantage on computer-use benchmarks — the skill needed to actually automate office work rather than just chat — is a direct challenge to how OpenAI and Anthropic are pricing their own agent products.

Microsoft's AI strategy is straining its own core businesses, report says

A Business Insider report describes 2026 as Satya Nadella's hardest year running Microsoft, arguing the company's aggressive AI push is putting real pressure on three of its most important businesses — Microsoft 365, GitHub, and Azure. Microsoft is spending a projected $190 billion on AI infrastructure this year but remains capacity-constrained, even as AI-native competitors chip away at its productivity and developer-tools franchises and internal restructuring adds strain on staff and leadership.

Why it matters: It's a reminder that even the company best positioned to sell AI picks-and-shovels is struggling to convert that position into comfort in its legacy businesses, which suggests the AI transition is disruptive even for its biggest winners.

Moonshot ships Kimi K3's open weights, the largest open-weight model ever released

Update. Moonshot AI published the full open weights for Kimi K3 — the 2.8-trillion-parameter model it unveiled two weeks ago — a day ahead of schedule, under an Apache 2.0 license on Hugging Face. The MXFP4-quantized download runs to roughly 594 gigabytes; the model uses a hybrid linear-attention mechanism (Kimi Delta Attention), handles a 1-million-token context window, and natively understands images and video. Moonshot recommends serving it on nodes with at least 64 accelerators.

Why it matters: Going from announcement to a downloadable, permissively licensed 2.8-trillion-parameter model in two weeks — bigger than anything Meta, Mistral, or Alibaba has open-sourced — hands any well-resourced lab a frontier-class starting point for free, right as US labs lobby to restrict exactly this kind of release.

Celeris-1 uses diffusion instead of autoregression to hit near-GPT-5 intelligence at 15x the speed

Celeris-1 is a new general-purpose language model that its creators say delivers near-GPT-5-level intelligence while responding roughly 15 times faster than comparable models. Rather than generating text token-by-token in the usual autoregressive way, it uses a diffusion-based inference architecture, posting a median response latency of 157 milliseconds and throughput of 1,280 tokens per second. Full benchmarks and a technical writeup accompanied the release.

Why it matters: Diffusion-based text generation has mostly been a research curiosity; a model claiming frontier-adjacent quality at production speed is early evidence the architecture could become a real alternative to autoregression for latency-sensitive agent workloads.

Amazon makes its largest-ever donation to a research group building mathematically verified AI agents

Amazon is providing substantial, long-term funding to the Lean Focused Research Organization — the largest single donation in the FRO's history. Lean is a programming language for machine-checked mathematical proofs, and Amazon argues it can be used to make AI agents' behavior formally verifiable rather than just empirically tested. Amazon is funding the work as an independent, open effort outside the company, saying that strengthens trust with customers, auditors, and regulators.

Why it matters: Most AI safety work today relies on testing and evals that sample behavior; funding formal-proof tooling is a bet that some agent guarantees eventually need to be mathematically certain, not just statistically likely.

Publicly shared Claude conversations are turning up in Google and Bing search results

Shared Claude chat links have been showing up in Google and Bing search results, making conversations that contain resumes, business discussions, and code easier for strangers to discover. It wasn't a breach of Anthropic's systems — the chats were already accessible to anyone with the link — but the incident highlights that users often treat a shareable link as private when it functions as a public webpage the moment it's indexed.

Why it matters: As AI chat exports become a normal way to share work, this is a preview of a recurring privacy failure mode: the gap between what a sharing feature technically does and what users assume it does.
