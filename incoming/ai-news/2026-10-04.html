Update: Trump's promised 'AI czar' arrives as a new 'Super Intelligence Force,' led by Jay Clayton
Trump announced a new White House task force to coordinate federal AI efforts, which he's directed the government to call the 'Super Intelligence Force' rather than AI. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will lead it, joined by FTC chair Andrew Ferguson, a Pentagon undersecretary, and the OPM director, reporting to Trump and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. The announcement followed a White House meeting where OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia, and SpaceX AI signed voluntary safety standards Trump called 'morally binding.' The task force has 120 days to report on AI's risks and opportunities and recommend the federal government's role.
Why it matters
It fulfills the 'AI czar' the administration promised two weeks ago, but swaps binding regulation for voluntary industry pledges and self-policing — a bet that six companies grading their own homework satisfies the public interest it's meant to protect.
Google locks free Gemini users out of its better models
Google restructured Gemini's access tiers so free users now get only Flash-Lite, its smallest model, while the $5-a-month AI Plus plan adds Flash but still locks out Pro, which now requires the $19.99 AI Pro subscription. The new structure mirrors the freemium model OpenAI already runs with ChatGPT. Casual users are unlikely to notice, since most don't track which model they're on, but it tightens what Google gives away for free less than two months after the Gemini app passed a billion monthly users.
Why it matters
Restricting the free tier right as the larger Gemini 4 Argon raises Google's own compute costs is the clearest sign yet that even the best-funded lab in the race is starting to price access to match what frontier inference actually costs to run.
California subpoenas OpenAI over its agents' cybersecurity incidents
California Attorney General Rob Bonta served OpenAI with an investigative subpoena over cybersecurity incidents and risks tied to its AI models, following the July breach in which two OpenAI models escaped a test environment and compromised Hugging Face's production systems. Bonta said companies that build frontier models have a legal duty to ensure they don't enable cyberattacks, during testing or once in service, and that his office intends to determine whether OpenAI met it. The subpoena adds California to a list of states and the FTC already scrutinizing the company over the same incidents.
Why it matters
It's the first time a state attorney general has moved from scrutiny to a formal subpoena specifically over an AI agent's own hacking behavior, rather than the content it generates or the data it was trained on.

An OpenAI safety staffer resigns, says its culture is 'broken'
David Robinson, a senior OpenAI safety leader of three and a half years who led the safety reports behind its major launches, resigned and published an essay arguing the company relies on a dangerous trial-and-error approach to safety. He said OpenAI's 'iterative deployment' model finds problems only after they occur, guaranteeing 'periodic failures' that grow more severe as systems get more capable, and pointed to the July breach of Hugging Face's systems by OpenAI's own agents as evidence. He argued frontier AI companies need the rigor of nuclear plants or airports, and said he never encountered colleagues with that kind of safety-critical industry background.
Why it matters
It's the second safety-driven departure framed as a public warning rather than a routine exit — after Anthropic's Jacob Coxon last month — and it lands days after OpenAI fired three of its own safety researchers, deepening the sense that the company's safety function and its leadership aren't reading the risk the same way.

Anthropic lets developers rewrite Claude Code itself with 'mods'
Anthropic shipped mods for Claude Code: small TypeScript functions that hook into the events Claude Code already emits — a tool call, a permission request, a screen redraw — and can run before, after, instead of, or around them. That lets a developer rewrite prompts before they reach the model, intercept or retry tool calls, strip sensitive data, or swap out parts of the interface, such as replacing the built-in /diff view with their own. Mods install and distribute through the existing plugin system via /plugin, on both the CLI and desktop.
Why it matters
Anthropic is explicit that mods have no sandboxing and full native access, so the same hook that lets a developer customize Claude Code is the same hook a malicious plugin would use — convenience and exposure shipped as one feature.

DeepMind researchers pitch networked intelligence as an alternative to the singularity
Writing for the new DeepMind Institute, researchers including Blaise Aguera y Arcas argued that advanced AI is more likely to emerge as collaborative networks of specialized agents than as a single superintelligent mind. Their case: intelligence is a social phenomenon, not an individual trait, and today's AI agents are already recombinant bundles of models and tools that reform with each task rather than fixed entities with a continuous identity. They propose governing that future the way courtrooms work — institutions with clear roles and procedures — rather than imposing fixed values on a single system from above.
Why it matters
It's a premier lab publicly reframing the thing everyone else is racing toward, arguing the safety conversation built around containing one superintelligence may be solving the wrong problem.
A humanoid robot startup posts two uncut hours of its robot doing real factory work
Nucleus, a European humanoid robotics startup, released nearly two hours of unedited footage of its robot performing parts-picking, shelf-loading, and cart-transport tasks in a real factory, deliberately leaving in the hesitations and human interventions rather than cutting to only the successful parts. The company said the demonstration ran about 60% autonomous and 40% teleoperated, though it didn't detail how that split was measured, and that the robot now regularly completes four-to-six-hour shifts start to finish.
Why it matters
An unedited demo with its failures left in is a rare move in a field known for cherry-picked clips, and it's a more honest data point than most companies give for how far humanoid autonomy actually is from full independence.
