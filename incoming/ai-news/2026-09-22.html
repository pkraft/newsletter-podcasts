Anthropic quietly tests Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 ahead of an expected release
Screenshots and outputs matching neither Claude Opus 5 nor Fable 5.1 began circulating on X over the weekend, showing a model producing detailed interactive animations and walking mascot sprites in pure JavaScript, drawn directly in code with no external image files or plugins. TestingCatalog reports the samples point to Anthropic quietly testing two unreleased models, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2, in the wild ahead of a planned announcement — possibly this week. Rumored pricing for Opus 5.5 is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, roughly matching Opus 5's structure. Anthropic has not commented on the tests or confirmed either model's existence.
Why it matters
Fable 5.1 shipped less than three weeks ago, so a live test of 5.2 alongside a next Opus points to Anthropic compressing its release cadence just as rivals ship their own upgrades same-day. Unconfirmed leaks are also how frontier labs increasingly telegraph capability before committing to a launch date or safety disclosure.

xAI ships Grok 4.7, a coding-focused update with new safety limits
xAI released Grok 4.7, built on a new, larger base model and aimed at longer coding and knowledge-work tasks through improved self-verification. The company says it matches Grok 4.6's speed while pricing API access at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens. Grok 4.7 scores 46.3% on CursorBench 4.0 and 71% on DeepSWE v1.1, and xAI highlights stronger safety features in cybersecurity tasks, including limits on executing risky commands autonomously.
Why it matters
Grok 4.7 is xAI's second coding-oriented release since summer and lands the same week rivals are testing their own next flagships, keeping pressure on price-per-token even as the model adds guardrails around autonomous command execution — a category where several labs have recently disclosed real breaches during testing.

Xiaomi open-sources MiMo-V2.6, an omnimodal model aimed at agentic and robotics tasks
Xiaomi released open-weight MiMo-V2.6 Pro and Flash models that coordinate agents to build and visually verify interactive 3D scenes, create Blender assets, control a robotic arm from camera feeds, assemble videos, and compose music as scores or MIDI. The models are available now in Xiaomi's AI Studio, MiMo Code, MiMo Desktop, its MiMo API Platform, and OpenRouter, alongside a published technical report, training environments, and reinforcement-learning code. Xiaomi says reinforcement learning is the central training paradigm behind the release, and that it kept training stable at the scale needed by freezing the model's mixture-of-experts router and building multi-layer defenses against reward hacking during RL.
Why it matters
A consumer electronics company shipping an open-weight omnimodal agent model — not just a phone assistant — shows how far model-building has spread beyond the dedicated labs, and the open training details make MiMo-V2.6 unusually reproducible compared to most agentic releases this year.

SoftBank launches an $11 billion bond sale to fund its next OpenAI payment
SoftBank Group launched $10 billion in dollar-denominated senior unsecured notes and €1 billion in euro notes to fund the third tranche of its follow-on investment in OpenAI, a $10 billion payment expected to close October 1. Pricing is expected September 24 and settlement September 29; the new bonds will also retire a $10 billion bridge loan SoftBank had arranged earlier to cover the same commitment. If it closes at its intended size, the deal would be the largest non-financial corporate bond offering ever out of Asia-Pacific and Japan, surpassing 7-Eleven's $10.93 billion raise in January 2021. Once the tranche closes, SoftBank's cumulative investment in OpenAI is expected to reach roughly $64.6 billion for about a 13% stake.
Why it matters
SoftBank is now borrowing at record scale to keep funding a single OpenAI position, on top of nearly $15 billion in note issuance this year alone — more than any other speculative-grade borrower in global bond markets. That concentration ties SoftBank's own credit profile increasingly tightly to OpenAI's trajectory, well beyond a typical venture stake.

OpenAI forms a mathematician advisory group after its Navier-Stokes claim faced a credit dispute
OpenAI created the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, hosted at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study, with nine founding mathematicians including Timothy Gowers, Edward Witten, and Melanie Matchett Wood. The group will advise on reviewing and communicating AI-generated math results and on academic standards, but OpenAI says it won't influence the company's research pace; members receive no OpenAI funding, can criticize the company publicly, and control their own membership. The move follows OpenAI's September 8 claim that an internal model resolved the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem and more than 100 other open problems, and a subsequent dispute over whether OpenAI pressured a mathematician to drop an Anthropic-employed coauthor from credit on a related paper. The Clay Mathematics Institute has said only that the underlying proof "apparently" holds up, not that it has completed formal review.
Why it matters
An advisory board with no funding ties and public-criticism rights is a real check, but it only reviews how OpenAI's math claims get communicated — not whether the underlying proofs hold up, which is still the Clay Institute's and the broader math community's call. The credit dispute that prompted this is still unresolved.

Google opens preorders for a $899 laptop built around Gemini
Google opened preorders on September 21 for the Googlebook, an $899 Android-based laptop designed around Gemini rather than Chrome OS or a traditional desktop metaphor. Features include Magic Cursor, which lets users point at on-screen content and hand it to Gemini, and Rambler, which cleans up rough voice dictation into polished text, alongside a set of AI-powered widgets. The device ships with 12 months of Google AI Pro included and begins shipping October 4 in the US. Google already places roughly 50 million Chromebooks in US schools, and the Googlebook gives it a path to move that education install base toward a Gemini-first machine over time.
Why it matters
This is Google's first hardware bet that a laptop's main job is running an AI assistant rather than a browser or apps — and its existing Chromebook footprint in schools gives it a distribution channel most AI hardware entrants, including OpenAI's own device plans, don't have.
 
04  Tools now with fewer humans
1 source  marktechpost.com
AWS open-sources Strands Harness, an agent that runs on any cloud or model
AWS released Strands Harness, an open-source, ready-to-run AI agent with built-in web search, shell access, memory, and file editing, published under the Apache 2.0 license with first Python and TypeScript releases. Unlike AWS's own Bedrock-tied tools, Strands Harness runs on frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or Bedrock, and deploys locally or across any cloud, including Google Cloud, Azure, Modal, and Cloudflare, not only AWS. AWS says the harness addresses a common gap: developers can prototype agents locally with tools like Claude Code or Codex but struggle to move that same setup into a scalable cloud deployment. Across six benchmarks, AWS reports Strands Harness used 28% fewer tokens than running the same Claude or GPT models directly, at comparable accuracy.
Why it matters
AWS is positioning itself as the neutral runtime layer for agents built on competitors' models rather than pushing a model of its own — a bet that infrastructure, not the underlying LLM, is where it can win share as coding agents commoditize. The token-efficiency claim, if it holds up independently, matters more than the open-source license itself.
