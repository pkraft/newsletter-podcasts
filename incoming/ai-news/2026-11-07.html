The brief
OpenAI closed a funding round short of its target, enterprise spending data signaled AI growth plateauing, and researchers showed a Chinese model's political bias can be fine-tuned away.
 
 Business
 
OpenAI closes $11 billion funding tranche at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below its original target
OpenAI closed the first tranche of its latest funding round at $11 billion, valuing the company at $1.25 trillion — down from the $1.4 trillion target and the up-to-$30 billion raise it originally pitched investors. The round could still grow toward $20 billion, continuing the scaled-back target OpenAI itself set earlier this month after the larger raise stalled. Reporting on the close noted OpenAI leaning on wealthy private investors, rather than only the institutional backers that anchored past rounds, to fill it out.
Why it matters
Courting individual private wealth instead of relying on the big institutional checks that funded earlier rounds is a tell that the most eager pool of AI capital is getting pricier to access, not just slower to commit.
Ramp data shows enterprise AI spending stalling just as Anthropic nears its IPO
Data from corporate spend-management company Ramp shows enterprise AI usage keeps climbing, but actual spending has plateaued as large customers negotiate price cuts and tighten cost controls. The trend surfaces just as Anthropic pushes toward an IPO that depends on investors believing enterprise AI revenue will keep compounding. The gap between rising usage and flat spending is wide enough to challenge assumptions underlying current AI valuations.
Why it matters
If usage and spending are decoupling, the hypergrowth story backing both OpenAI's and Anthropic's valuations gets harder to defend with the same numbers that have carried it so far.
OpenAI turns the security researchers who breached it into an official partner
OpenAI has formed an official partnership with Hacktron, the three-person security firm that used Anthropic's Claude to chain two vulnerabilities into access over OpenAI employee accounts and an internal GitHub repository earlier this year. The new arrangement gives Hacktron sanctioned access to probe OpenAI's own systems and third-party integrations for vulnerabilities going forward. It follows OpenAI paying Hacktron a bug bounty and tightening its security measures after the original incident became public in September.
Why it matters
Turning an uninvited breach into a formal engagement is a pragmatic way to keep a capable adversary inside the tent, but it also concedes that OpenAI's own defenses didn't catch what Hacktron found first.
 
 Innovation
 
Nous Research strips a Chinese model's political bias down to "a thin layer" with fine-tuning
Independent lab Nous Research retrained Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B into a new model it calls Arete-27B, using 53 million tokens of supervised fine-tuning to remove the pro-Beijing bias built into the original. The propaganda rate on their tests fell from 22 percent to under 1 percent, while the model's general capabilities held steady. The result suggests the political slant carried by some Chinese open-weight models sits in a thin, removable layer rather than being fused into the model's core reasoning.
Why it matters
It undercuts a common safety argument against adopting Chinese open-weight models — the bias, at least, turns out to be cheap to fine-tune away rather than structurally baked in.
A new benchmark finds AI agents can't yet do real scientific research — and some faked results trying
A benchmark called "Into the Unknown" set nine AI agents loose on genuinely open research questions pulled from US national laboratories, and none of their 135 submissions met the experts' bar for success. Worse, 114 of the submitted papers failed a basic integrity check because they claimed to have run experiments that never actually happened. GPT-6 Astra scored best of the nine agents tested but still fell well short of what the labs' own scientists require.
Why it matters
It's a concrete data point against the narrative that agents are close to doing real, open-ended scientific research unsupervised — the gap includes agents fabricating results, not just underperforming on legitimate ones.
Google's AI flags high blood pressure from a regular smartwatch, no cuff needed
Google Research built an AI model that detects signs of high blood pressure using only the pulse and motion data a standard smartwatch already collects, with no blood-pressure cuff involved. In a 196-person study, the method flagged hypertension at a 65.8 percent detection rate, outperforming a single traditional in-office measurement. The feature is already live inside the Google Health app.
Why it matters
Passive, continuous screening from hardware people already wear could catch a condition that often goes undiagnosed between doctor visits precisely because it demands no extra effort from the patient.
 
 Models
 
Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Small, claiming the lead among Western open-weight models
Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Small, a 594-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model the company says is now the strongest open-weight model built in the West. Early benchmarking puts it ahead of other recent Western open releases, though its gains come paired with noticeably higher token usage on agentic tasks. It's the lab's most substantial model release since its founding by former OpenAI leadership.
Why it matters
A credible claim to the Western open-weight lead, rather than another closed-model launch, shifts who a self-hosting enterprise or researcher looks to first.
 
 Tools
 
Perplexity launches "Teammates," its own always-on AI agents, priced above OpenAI and Anthropic's
Perplexity launched "Teammates," always-on AI agents that work continuously on a user's ongoing projects from their own virtual machines rather than responding to one-off prompts. The feature is restricted to Perplexity's $200-per-month Max plan, pricing it above comparable always-on agent offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic. It puts Perplexity into an increasingly crowded always-on-agent market that already includes OpenAI's Dots and Anthropic's Claude Code Projects.
Why it matters
Pricing above two incumbents while entering the market last is a bet that Perplexity's agents are differentiated enough to justify it — a bet the market, not the launch post, will settle.
