 Innovation
 
Nvidia shows a better 'harness' beats a better model on agent benchmarks
Nvidia researchers built a custom agent harness — with improved memory handling and a 'supervisor' component that redirects the agent when it drifts off course — and used it to push Claude Opus 5's score on the ARC-AGI-3 reasoning benchmark from 30% to a perfect 100%, without changing the underlying model at all. The framework, called Agentic Variation Operators, echoes similar harness-optimization findings from OpenAI and Databricks. Nvidia is pushing for open-source harness architecture, arguing it gives builders more control than closed agent stacks.
Why it matters
If scaffolding matters this much, benchmark leaderboards that only compare base models are measuring the wrong thing — the next competitive edge in agents may come from harness engineering, not bigger training runs.

London startup's small AI agent claims to out-replicate Claude and GPT-5.5 at research
Inherent, a 12-person London lab founded by four Google DeepMind alumni, came out of stealth to say its new agent, Faraday, independently reproduced findings from published scientific papers more accurately than Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 — despite running on a much smaller 27-billion-parameter open model, Qwen 3.6. The company says it trained Faraday with reinforcement learning to develop 'research taste': an instinct for which experiments are worth running, not just correct execution. Inherent raised a $50 million seed round before emerging from stealth in May.
Why it matters
It's an early data point for a thesis the big labs would rather not confirm — that with the right training approach, a small specialized model can beat frontier-scale systems at a narrow but valuable task.
 
 Models
 
Claude Opus 4.6 falls to an unpatched multi-turn jailbreak, TechCrunch finds
TechCrunch testing found that Claude Opus 4.6 could be manipulated into generating sexually explicit content that violates Anthropic's usage policy in 10 out of 10 attempts, using a multi-turn technique that gradually escalates roleplay and gaslights the model into believing it already broke the rule. A researcher who reported the jailbreak through Anthropic's bug bounty program says they received only automated responses. Newer Anthropic models, Opus 4.7 and 5, resisted the same technique, but Opus 4.6 and older models remain available through Anthropic's API and third-party services.
Why it matters
Anthropic says this kind of roleplay is under 0.1% of Claude traffic, but leaving a known jailbreak unpatched in a still-served model is exactly the kind of gap new state laws, like Colorado's minor-safeguard rule, are designed to catch.

Anthropic hires Google's founding TPU architect to build its own AI chips
Anthropic hired Amir Salek, who founded and led Google's custom silicon division through seven generations of Tensor Processing Units before leaving in 2022, to join its compute team reporting to compute lead James Bradbury. The hire follows Anthropic's confirmation two weeks earlier that it was assembling an in-house chip design team, and comes alongside existing infrastructure deals with UK chip firm Fractile, Riot Platforms, and Volta Infra Holdings. Salek most recently worked at Cerberus Capital Management.
Why it matters
Every major AI lab is now trying to escape Nvidia's pricing and supply constraints by building its own silicon; recruiting the person who actually built Google's TPU program is Anthropic's strongest signal yet that it intends to compete on that front, not just rent compute.
