... Alibaba has released something called kwen-Drive one point zero. It is an autonomous driving system. It is built on their Qwen3.5-4B language model. They have added modules for bird’s-eye-view 3D mapping and route planning. The idea is to unify the perception for driving with the cockpit assistant. Instead of running them as separate systems, they are trying to keep it all in one model. They used reinforcement learning for the training. In simulation, this cut the road-veering errors from twenty-four percent down to twelve percent. That is a reduction, I suppose. The model is free for researchers to access on Hugging Face, ModelScope, and GitHub. However, there is a catch. Alibaba’s own testing found that the model’s stated reasons for a driving decision often do not match what actually caused it. It can describe a 3D scene. But it cannot reliably understand the space it is describing. So, it tells you why it is braking, but you shouldn't expect the explanation to match the maneuver. ... The reason this matters is that explainability was supposed to be one of the safety advantages. It was thought that language-model-based driving systems would be better than black-box perception stacks. kwen-Drive's gap between decisions and explanations shows that this advantage doesn't arrive for free. Just because a model can produce fluent language about what it sees, doesn't mean it understands anything. This report comes from The Decoder. ...

Cloudflare and open A-I have automated vulnerability triage and patching. They call it Daybreak. Cloudflare launched a new tool inside its Managed Defense service. It pairs open A-I’s Daybreak security models with Cloudflare’s own WAF and traffic data. The goal is to sort through the thousands of findings a scan can surface. In Cloudflare’s example, they had four thousand vulnerabilities. Seventy-eight of them were critical. The system narrows it down to the handful that are actually deployed. And exploitable. And under attack. The service can propose patches. And temporary WAF rules. But every remediation step stays under customer control. It’s currently invite-only. Why it matters. Vulnerability scanners have long buried security teams in findings without production context. Pairing a frontier cyber model with a network operator’s real traffic data helps say which vulnerabilities matter right now. It’s a narrow, well-scoped use of critical AI capability. open A-I needs this to work if it wants to keep defending Astra’s release.
