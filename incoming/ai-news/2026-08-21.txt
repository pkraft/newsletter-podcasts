Anthropic is doing a bit of tidying up. They have taken four separate ingredients. Computer use. Browser access. Skills. And files. They are now bundling them into one surface. It is for building production agents. According to their announcement, these capabilities are generally available. A team can upload a procedure once. They can pin a version. And they can reuse file IDs across requests. This means no more repeating browser round trips. It is just one operational surface now. This is a signal of maturity, I suppose. They are treating agent-building as a product category. It has stable primitives. It is not a grab-bag of betas anymore. Just a slightly more organized mess. This comes from the TLDR AI newsletter. Google has done something. They’ve added their Antigravity agent platform to eligible gemin-eye Enterprise subscriptions. Administrators get sandbox, tool-permission, budget, identity, and audit controls to manage it. The point is, they’re folding Antigravity into enterprise plans and existing editors. They’re turning their expense-management expertise toward AI inference with a service called Router. It’s a U.S.-only service for switching among models from open A-I, Anthropic, DeepSeek, xAI, and others through one A-P-I. Routing is based on benchmarks, cost, or task difficulty. It’s free through the end of two thousand twenty-six and comes with a twenty-six dollar launch credit. An expense-management company shipping a model router is a sign of how commoditized model selection has become. Routing logic and cost, not any single model, are now the product.
