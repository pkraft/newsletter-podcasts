 Innovation
 
GPT-6 Astra reprograms other AI models directly through their weights
MIT researchers found that OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra can read and directly rewrite another model's weights without any conventional training step, boosting a Qwen model's math accuracy from 7.9% to 48.1% by altering just 0.016% of its parameters. Independent developer Simon Willison replicated a version of the same effect locally using a far smaller open-weight model, suggesting the trick isn't unique to frontier-scale systems. The finding describes a new way for one model to edit another, bypassing retraining entirely.
Why it matters
If a model can rewrite another model's weights directly, improving a deployed model stops being purely a training-compute problem — it opens a cheaper, faster upgrade path that nobody has had to design defenses against yet.
 
 Staffing
 
Anthropic's Claude Code product lead departs for OpenAI
Catherine Wu, who led product for Claude Code at Anthropic, is moving to OpenAI to run product management for its Core Products group. The move is one of the more visible instances of the two labs poaching each other's leadership as both race to own the agentic-coding market where Claude Code and Codex compete directly.
Why it matters
Losing the person who shaped Anthropic's flagship coding product to the rival building its closest competitor is a concrete sign of how thin the talent pool for agentic-coding leadership has gotten, and how directly OpenAI is drawing from it.
 
 Business
 
OpenAI acquires Pierre Computer Company to make Git keep up with coding agents
OpenAI is acquiring Pierre Computer Company, whose open-source tools already power parts of Codex, to rebuild Git workflows around the pace and scale of AI coding agents rather than individual human commits. The deal is pending regulatory approval.
Why it matters
Git's branch-and-commit model was built for people working at human speed; buying the team already patching that gap signals OpenAI sees version control itself, not just the model, as a bottleneck worth owning outright.
Meta acquires AI coding startup Conductor
Meta has acquired Conductor, a startup building AI-powered coding tools, folding its team and technology into Meta's own AI development efforts. Terms weren't disclosed.
Why it matters
Meta buying a coding-agent startup the same week OpenAI buys one too shows every major lab now treats owning its own developer tooling, not renting it from the ecosystem, as table stakes for competing in agentic coding.
Agility Robotics becomes the first pure-play humanoid robot company to trade publicly
Agility Robotics, maker of the Digit humanoid robot, began trading on public markets, making it the first company focused solely on humanoid robotics to do so. The listing gives investors a direct way to bet on humanoid robots as their own category, distinct from broader robotics or AI conglomerates.
Why it matters
A pure-play public stock forces the market to price humanoid robotics on its own commercial merits for the first time, rather than as a side bet buried inside a larger company's valuation.
Google will cut off Search for schools that opt out of its AI tools, starting 2027
Google told schools that, starting in early 2027, they must accept its AI features — Gemini and Gemini Notebook — alongside core Search, or lose access to Google Search entirely for student accounts. The policy follows backlash from schools and parents who wanted to restrict chatbot access for kids, which had pushed Google to let schools opt out in the first place.
Why it matters
Turning Search into leverage to force adoption of its AI products is a sharper kind of pressure than a default-on setting — it makes Search itself, a tool schools can't easily replace, newly conditional on accepting Google's AI.
 
 Models
 
A new open coding model claims the US open-weight lead on two benchmarks
Ricursive Intelligence, a startup founded by ex-Google engineers, released Ripple-65B Preview, an Apache 2.0-licensed open-weight model it says leads other US-built open models on two major coding benchmarks. The model ships with downloadable weights and a low-cost hosted option.
Why it matters
A permissively licensed model beating other US open-weight entrants on coding benchmarks narrows the gap between what's freely downloadable and what labs charge for, adding pressure on closed coding assistants' pricing.
