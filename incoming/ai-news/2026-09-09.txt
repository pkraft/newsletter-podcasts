There is a new release from Google deep mind. It is called the AlphaGenome Atlas. It is a predictive map of every possible DNA mutation in the human genome. The dataset is one petabyte in size. That is about thirty times the size of the AlphaFold database. It contains precomputed predictions for all nine billion possible single-letter DNA variants. Each variant gets a score. It is called the AlphaGenome Variant Impact score, or AVI. This score combines two of deep mind's models into a single ranking. It works across both protein-coding and non-coding regions. There is also a catalogue of more than two thousand five hundred sequence motifs. Early collaborators say the atlas has already surfaced disease-causing variants. Standard methods missed these. The atlas also identified twenty-two percent more genetic associations than traditional approaches. Why does this matter? AlphaFold turned protein structure into a lookup instead of a multi-year experiment. Doing the same for the functional effect of any possible mutation could meaningfully shorten the diagnostic search. This is particularly for rare-disease patients whose cause is currently unknown. The source is Google deep mind, with reporting from The Decoder. ...
