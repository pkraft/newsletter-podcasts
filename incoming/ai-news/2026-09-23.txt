All right. Next item. Google Research has published a paper. It is called RRSI. Or rather, that is the acronym. The full title is Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses. It is a fix for a specific problem. You know how AI agents can improve themselves? Well, they do it by memorizing their own tests. See, a "harness" is just the wrapper. The prompts. The tools. The memory. The control flow around the model. Recently, these harnesses have been allowed to edit themselves. They propose changes. They select changes. They improve. But Google found a flaw. It overfits. It gets better at the training tasks. But when you test it on new things? The gains shrink. Or vanish. It is like studying for a test by memorizing the answers. You pass the test. You fail in real life. So RRSI puts constraints on it. It limits how many edits a candidate can make at once. It favors unexplored changes. It prunes the edits that are too small or too costly to be useful. They tested it across eight benchmarks. Coding. Agentic tasks. Engineering design. The result? Out-of-distribution performance improved by up to four point seven points. And it cut the token use by thirty percent. Why does this matter? Because self-improving agents are a direct path to better systems without retraining the whole model. But overfitting has been the main reason those gains haven't held up outside the lab. A method that transfers, and uses fewer tokens doing it? That is a step toward self-improvement that is measured on more than just its own test set. It is in arXiv. Titled RRSI. Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses. ...
