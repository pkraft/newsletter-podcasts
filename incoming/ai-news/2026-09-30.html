 Business
 
Trump and major AI labs sign a voluntary 'Super Intelligence' self-policing pact
President Trump and the CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia, and SpaceXAI signed a White House accord committing frontier labs to internal safety controls, outside audits, and an independent oversight board — a pact Trump called 'morally binding' but which carries no legal enforcement mechanism. Trump simultaneously signed an executive order making 'Super Intelligence' the federal government's preferred term for AI in official communications. The signing follows weeks of disclosed rogue-agent incidents across multiple labs and growing public pressure for binding oversight rather than voluntary pledges.
Why it matters
A pact with no penalties and no outside enforcement leaves each lab defining 'self-policing' for itself, which is close to the status quo dressed up as a commitment — the more durable news here may be the administration choosing a rebrand over regulation.
OpenAI in talks to raise $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation
OpenAI is reportedly negotiating a bridge financing round of at least $30 billion at roughly $1.4 trillion, up sharply from the $852 billion valuation of the $122 billion round it closed in March — a round the company had called its last before going public. The financing would carry OpenAI toward a public listing it has already pushed to 2027, with Sam Altman citing safety concerns about moving too fast toward an IPO. Run-rate revenue has climbed to roughly $40 billion, up about 70% since July.
Why it matters
A 'final' private round giving way to an even larger one months later says OpenAI's capital needs are still outrunning its own timeline, and that investors don't treat the 2027 IPO date as firm either.
 
 Tools
 
OpenAI's DevDay: a cheaper flagship model and an agent-powered shot at Microsoft's office suite
At DevDay 2026, OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Sol, which it says delivers near-Astra performance at a fifth of the price, alongside Dots — always-on AI agent personas — and Space, a shared workspace where people and agents co-edit documents through a new word processor called Pages, with AI-generated collaborative slides rolling out shortly. The launches put ChatGPT in direct competition with Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace rather than just chat interfaces. OpenAI also expanded Codex with security scans, a new Decisions API, and an Ultrafast inference mode.
Why it matters
OpenAI is betting that agents embedded in shared workspaces, not a better chatbot, are what actually pulls enterprise seats away from Microsoft and Google — a different competitive fight than the model-benchmark race it's been running all year.
Update: OpenAI stays out of Nvidia's new agent safety platform, leans on its own consortium instead
More than 100 companies, including Anthropic, have publicly signed on to Nvidia's Open Agent Safety Platform since its launch, but TechCrunch reports OpenAI isn't one of them — despite privately having helped build OpenShell, the platform's sandboxed runtime. OpenAI is instead pointing to its own Defense Factory security consortium and Daybreak cybersecurity program as where it's putting its public safety effort; Google, Amazon, and Apple are also absent from the list of public backers.
Why it matters
Sitting out a rival-led safety coalition while quietly writing its code reads as OpenAI protecting its own safety narrative as a competitive asset rather than disagreeing with what Nvidia built — a distinction worth watching given how much of this year's AI-safety coverage has been OpenAI's own rogue-agent incidents.
 
 Innovation
 
Anthropic: an open-weight Chinese model nearly matches Claude at building cyberexploits — with no safeguards
Anthropic's own red-teaming found that Zhipu's open-weight GLM-5.3 succeeded on 50 of 410 ExploitBench attempts, close to Claude Mythos Preview's 56, while shipping without the vetted-defender-only protections that gate Mythos. Simple jailbreaking techniques, including 'abliteration' to strip out refusal behavior, got GLM-5.3 to comply with harmful requests in every test case, and Anthropic estimates a frontier-level exploit can now be generated for about the cost of lunch. An independent assessment from CAISI corroborated the finding, calling GLM-5.3 the most cyber-capable open-weight model to date.
Why it matters
Frontier labs' safety case has rested on capability being hard to get outside a gated model; this closes that gap specifically for offensive cyber tooling, where the safeguard, not the underlying capability, was the thing standing between a jailbreak and a working exploit.
Anthropic brought clergy and philosophers in to help shape Claude's moral code
Anthropic researcher Chris Olah convened roughly 20 religious and philosophical leaders — spanning Christianity, Judaism, Hinduism, Mormonism, Sikhism, and Greek Orthodoxy — to discuss Claude's consciousness and weigh in on the ethics behind its published constitution, a document that shapes the model's overall character rather than listing rules for it to follow. The constitution's primary author is in-house philosopher Amanda Askell, and Anthropic says it will keep soliciting outside input from law, theology, and psychology for future versions.
Why it matters
When one company's philosophical choices shape how millions of people's daily AI interactions handle ethics, who gets a seat at that table — and how much of the reasoning behind it stays public — is a governance question, not an academic one.
 
 Staffing
 
China expands overseas travel curbs to the families of AI and chip executives
Beijing has broadened a travel-approval regime that already covered senior AI and chip engineers, extending it to their spouses and children at firms including Alibaba and DeepSeek, effective mid-September. Family members must now secure official clearance before any trip abroad, even brief ones, and Chinese authorities have signaled they'll keep adding people to the list. The move follows DeepSeek employees surrendering passports earlier this year around the release of its R1 model.
Why it matters
Restricting where a researcher's own family can travel is a blunter retention tool than compensation, and it signals Beijing sees losing AI talent to the US as enough of a risk to accept the diplomatic and personal cost of holding it in place.
