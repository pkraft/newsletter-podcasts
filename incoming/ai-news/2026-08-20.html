 Business
 
Anthropic surpasses OpenAI in quarterly revenue for the first time
Anthropic posted $11.6 billion in second-quarter revenue, more than doubling its $4.73 billion in Q1 and edging past OpenAI's $6.7 billion for the same quarter — the first time Anthropic has out-earned OpenAI in a single quarter. OpenAI's revenue grew a slower 18% quarter-over-quarter, and its operating loss widened to $12.3 billion from $9.3 billion the prior quarter, according to a Wall Street Journal report. Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate has reached roughly $65 billion, against OpenAI's roughly $40 billion.
Why it matters
Revenue growth, not funding or valuation, is the metric investors are watching most closely ahead of both companies' expected IPOs, and a quarterly lead that flips from OpenAI to Anthropic — while OpenAI's losses grow rather than shrink — undercuts the assumption that OpenAI's head start makes it the default frontrunner.
Unitree's Shanghai IPO surges as much as 630%, valuing the humanoid robot maker near $50 billion
Chinese robotics maker Unitree raised about $904 million in its Shanghai Stock Exchange STAR Market debut, then saw shares surge as much as 630% intraday before closing up 460% — the first mainland China listing of a humanoid robot maker. The IPO was oversubscribed more than 8,000 times, and Chinese AI lab DeepSeek was among the investors, contributing roughly 140.8 million yuan. Founded in 2016 by Wang Xingxing, Unitree is known for humanoid and quadruped robots that run, dance, and do backflips.
Why it matters
A debut this oversubscribed, with a rival AI lab as a backer, signals how much domestic Chinese capital is chasing humanoid robotics specifically — a bet that AI's next commercial wave is embodied hardware, not just software, and a valuation frontier labs' own robotics efforts will now be measured against.
TerraPower to announce its first AI data center project this year
Nuclear startup TerraPower plans to announce its first data-center customer project in 2026, with construction targeted for 2027 — its second power plant after the Natrium reactor already under construction in Wyoming. The 345-megawatt molten-salt design stores excess heat in molten sodium, letting the plant absorb the rapid power swings of GPU workloads without the battery banks competing designs need. Meta agreed in January to buy eight Natrium plants from TerraPower, though the customer for this new project hasn't been named.
Why it matters
Nuclear has been AI's most talked-about, least-delivered power solution; a molten-salt design built specifically to handle GPU load swings, with a named construction timeline rather than a memorandum of understanding, is a more concrete test of whether nuclear can actually show up on the schedule AI capacity buildouts need.
 
 Innovation
 
OpenAI previews Private Safety Processing to match Anthropic on privacy without new data retention
OpenAI is testing a new architecture called Private Safety Processing with early enterprise customers, designed to let its automated systems flag misuse patterns across sessions while keeping full Zero Data Retention protections intact — the underlying prompts and responses stay inaccessible to OpenAI employees, and only a narrow safety signal is surfaced. The move directly targets Anthropic, whose Claude Opus 5 and Claude Mythos models require 30 days of session-data retention as "Covered Models," a policy that has frustrated enterprises with sensitive workloads. OpenAI plans a broader rollout and a technical white paper in September.
Why it matters
Data retention has become a genuine competitive lever between the two leading labs — OpenAI is betting automated pattern detection can deliver Anthropic's safety guarantees without the compliance cost of logging, and if the technique holds up under scrutiny, it pressures Anthropic to either match it or defend the retention requirement on safety grounds.
 
 Models
 
Ornith-1.5 open-weight models match Claude Opus 4.8 using a self-improvement training loop
Ornith released Ornith-1.5, an open-weight model family in 397B, 35B, and 9B sizes, trained by having earlier versions of the model propose their own tasks, build task-specific scaffolding, and generate the solution rollouts used for reinforcement learning — extending the "self-scaffolding" approach of Ornith-1.0 into a closed self-improvement loop. The flagship 397B model scores 86.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 56.0 on DeepSWE, roughly matching Claude Opus 4.8 (85.0 and 59.0) and outperforming similarly sized open models like GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
Why it matters
The "self-improvement" describes the training procedure, not a live-updating model on your machine — but a comparatively unknown lab producing an open model that matches a frontier closed model's coding benchmarks is a sharper test of whether open weights are closing the gap than another incremental release from a name-brand lab would be.
 
 Tools
 
Meta's Muse Video model enters closed beta with native audio
Meta Superintelligence Labs' Muse Video, its first AI video generation model, is now in closed beta and producing 10-second clips with native, synchronized audio rather than silent video requiring a separate audio pass. Early samples show strong detail and temporal consistency, and the model already ranks third on the Arena text-to-video leaderboard behind OpenAI's and Google's leading models. It remains an early preview; general public access hasn't opened yet.
Why it matters
Native audio-video generation removes a step every other video model still requires — bolting on a separate audio model afterward — and Meta reaching third place puts real competitive pressure on Sora and Veo in a category Meta had lagged in until Muse Image launched a month ago.
