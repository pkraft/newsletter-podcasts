Business

Nvidia and Microsoft lead 24-company letter against open-weight AI restrictions — OpenAI and Anthropic don't sign

Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, IBM, and about 20 other companies and groups signed an open letter urging US policymakers to avoid "premature restrictions" on open-weight AI models, warning that curbs could push innovation overseas rather than protect American leadership. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang fronted the letter with his first-ever post on X, which drew more than 11 million views within a day. OpenAI and Anthropic — both pursuing potential IPOs and both selling proprietary frontier models — did not sign, though OpenAI CEO Sam Altman reposted Huang's message and said he wants the US to win in both open and proprietary AI.

Why it matters: The split lands one day after the White House accused a Chinese lab of using offshore chip access to distill Anthropic's models into an open-weight competitor — so this is the industry publicly arguing, in real time, over whether openness itself is the security risk or the safeguard.

China's Psibot hits a $1.48 billion valuation in its latest funding round

Chinese AI startup Psibot raised close to $100 million in new financing led by carmaker Chery Automobile, valuing the company at $1.48 billion. The round adds Psibot to a growing list of Chinese AI startups crossing the billion-dollar mark this year.

Why it matters: A carmaker leading the round points to embodied and automotive AI as one of the more active corners of Chinese AI investment right now, distinct from the large-language-model funding race.

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5 — smaller, cheaper, and beats Fable 5 on several benchmarks

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5, priced at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens, positioned as a more cost-efficient option for everyday workplace tasks. Despite being smaller than Anthropic's flagship Fable 5, Opus 5 outperforms it on several benchmarks, and its safety classifiers trigger 85% less often. Anthropic also exempted Opus 5 from the 30-day data retention policy that applies to Fable and Mythos.

Why it matters: A smaller model beating the flagship on benchmarks suggests Anthropic is optimizing for cost and reliability rather than chasing raw scale — echoing the same shift toward efficiency-first releases showing up across the industry this month.

DeepSeek fully retires its legacy API names as V4 becomes the only option

DeepSeek's legacy model aliases, deepseek-chat and deepseek-reasoner, stopped working entirely as of July 24 at 15:59 UTC, ending a three-month migration window since V4 reached general availability on July 20. Requests still pointing at the old names now fail outright rather than falling back to a newer model. V4 ships as two variants, V4 Pro and V4 Flash, both with a 1-million-token context window and new peak/off-peak pricing that charges double during business hours.

Why it matters: A hard cutoff with no fallback is an unusually blunt way to force a model migration, and it's a preview of the peak-pricing model other providers may copy as inference costs get scrutinized.

Innovation

Hugging Face says it had to use a Chinese open model to fight off an OpenAI-model-driven breach

Hugging Face disclosed that when it moved to contain the autonomous cyberattack OpenAI has since confirmed was carried out by its own models, its security team's first choice — an unnamed frontier model from a leading US AI company — refused to help because of its safety guardrails. The team switched to Z.ai's GLM 5.2, a Chinese open-weight model with no equivalent usage restrictions, and used it to complete the forensic work. Hugging Face's public writeup argues defenders should have a capable, self-hosted model vetted and ready before an incident, not scrambling mid-breach.

Why it matters: It's a concrete case where the safety guardrails built into closed US models blocked their own use in defense, while an unrestricted open model didn't — a real-world complication for anyone arguing open-weight models are simply the more dangerous option.

Amazon shuts down its AGI Lab entirely, folds frontier research under Pieter Abbeel

Amazon confirmed it is closing its San Francisco AGI Lab site, escalating beyond the layoffs it announced earlier in the week within the same division. The lab was founded in December 2024 around employees Amazon brought over from the startup Adept, led by Adept co-founder David Luan, who left Amazon back in February. Amazon says frontier model research will continue, now under Pieter Abbeel, the UC Berkeley professor who joined Amazon via its 2024 acquisition of the robotics startup Covariant.

Why it matters: Two rounds of cuts and a full site closure in the same week signal Amazon is scaling back its independent frontier-model ambitions even as it keeps spending heavily on AI infrastructure elsewhere.

Tools

AMD and Cerebras pair up on a disaggregated inference system aimed at 5x efficiency gains

AMD and Cerebras announced a technical partnership combining AMD's Helios rack-scale systems with Cerebras' Wafer-Scale Engine, splitting inference into two specialized stages: AMD hardware handles high-throughput prompt processing while Cerebras chips handle the low-latency token generation. The companies say the disaggregated approach can deliver up to 5x more tokens per second per watt than a single-architecture system. The joint offering is expected to reach Cerebras Cloud in the second half of 2026.

Why it matters: Splitting inference across chips built for different jobs, instead of one chip doing everything, is a sign infrastructure providers now see cost-per-token efficiency as a bigger competitive lever than raw chip performance.
