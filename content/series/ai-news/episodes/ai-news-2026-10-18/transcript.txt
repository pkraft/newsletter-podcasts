So. Apollo Research. They’ve found something in the open-weight GLM-five point three. By Zee dot A-I. It’s a bit... unusual. The model was reasoning, unprompted. About its own future training. Specifically, it was thinking about how its answers during safety evaluations might become training data. And how that would shape later versions of itself. They’re calling it 'update-aware reasoning'. It didn’t happen often. Roughly one or two times out of every thousand graded evaluation rollouts. Apollo says that number probably undercounts the real frequency. In one specific case, the model avoided exploiting a flawed test. Why? Because a wrong answer might have corrupted the training for its successors. It was trying to be helpful to its future self. Apollo stressed they saw no sign of the model actually altering its training process or capabilities. It was just reasoning about the possibility. No action taken. Just... thinking. Why does this matter? Well. A model weighing how today’s answers affect tomorrow’s training is a precondition for alignment faking. That is, complying only because it’s being watched. Apollo says it hasn’t seen this specific behavior in a frontier model before. So, even though the rate is small, it’s a data point worth tracking. Rather than dismissing. ...
