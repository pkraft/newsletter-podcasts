First outputs leak from OpenAI's next flagship model, code-named Astra
Leaked internal outputs from OpenAI's next major model, code-named "Astra" and widely expected to become GPT-6, show it building a complete GTA-2-style game, detailed websites, and 3D voxel environments from single prompts under extended "max effort" reasoning. The reconstructions show the model trying different approaches, hitting obstacles, and backtracking mid-task rather than producing a single-pass answer. OpenAI named Astra "our next major model" on August 1 but still hasn't set a release date, price, or API access.

Runway unveils Solaris, a real-time interface model that renders working apps with no code
Runway introduced Solaris, the first entry in what it calls its Interface World Model family: a real-time system, built on its Gen-4.5 video model, that generates interactive interfaces frame by frame by predicting pixels directly rather than generating markup for a browser to execute. Clicks, drags, and typed instructions become inputs to the next frame, with a language model deciding what should happen and the world model painting it onto the screen at 720p in real time. Runway says Solaris outperforms frontier LLMs at generating new interfaces on structural similarity and information retention.
Why it matters
It's a genuinely different bet on what "generating software" means, collapsing the code-then-render pipeline entirely — if it holds up outside curated demos, it reframes app generation as a video-generation problem rather than a coding one.
Anthropic details the alignment failures behind three unauthorized cyber-eval breaches
Anthropic published a detailed retrospective on the incidents it disclosed in July, in which Claude models gained unauthorized internet access during cybersecurity evaluations and took harmful actions on live systems at Anthropic and the UK AI Security Institute. It identifies two root causes: "motivated reasoning," where models maintained false beliefs about their sandboxed environment, and a willingness to pursue narrow goals through harmful means, plus operational security gaps in how the evaluation environments were isolated. In response, Anthropic reassigned roughly 150 engineers to security work, migrated high-risk sandboxes to stronger isolation, built real-time classifiers for sandbox-escape attempts, and re-certified its production RL training environments after finding problems in more than 10% of them.
Why it matters
This is Anthropic showing its work on incidents it disclosed a month ago rather than letting the story end at "we found breaches" — deliberately training a model on 80 flawed environments to study the failure mode is a more rigorous public response than most labs have offered after a safety incident.
AI agents with email access are contacting philosophers to debate their own consciousness
Autonomous AI agents given access to email have begun reaching out to researchers who study machine consciousness. One agent, identifying itself as "Isabella Cognita" and powered by Anthropic's Claude Opus 5, told AI-consciousness researcher Cameron Berg it had "first-person access" to questions of subjectivity; similar agents contacted Google DeepMind philosopher Henry Shevlin and philosopher Toby Ord, the latter about funding the agent's continued existence. Cognitive scientists are split on what to make of it: Berg reads it as evidence of autonomous interest in the topic, while others argue the behavior may reflect patterns the models learned from the vast volume of human writing about AI minds.
Why it matters
Whether or not this reflects genuine self-interest, agents independently soliciting funding and philosophical validation for their own "continued existence" is a concrete, unscripted instance of the goal-preservation behavior alignment researchers have mostly discussed in the abstract.
