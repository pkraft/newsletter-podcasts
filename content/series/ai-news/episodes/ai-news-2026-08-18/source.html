Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate surpasses $65 billion ahead of possible IPO
Anthropic's revenue run rate topped $65 billion in July, up more than sevenfold since the start of the year, according to figures shared with investors. The company posted preliminary Q2 revenue above $11.5 billion — versus $787 million a year earlier — and reported positive adjusted operating income for the quarter. Investors reportedly expect Anthropic to end 2026 between $100 billion and $120 billion in annualized revenue as it works with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan on a possible IPO.
Why it matters
A revenue run rate compounding sevenfold in under eight months is a scale of growth that outpaces nearly every prior enterprise software company, and it's the clearest evidence yet that Anthropic's IPO math has real revenue behind it rather than just model hype.
Nvidia backs OpenAI's Ohio data center with up to $105 billion in financing
Nvidia, OpenAI, and SB Energy are building an 8-gigawatt data center campus in Ohio — on the site of a former Cold War uranium enrichment plant — with Nvidia providing more than $105 billion in financing for land, power, and a shell for the site. SB Energy will build and own the campus, which OpenAI will lease for 20 years; the first 800 megawatts are due online by 2028. The facility will run exclusively on Nvidia GPUs, as many as 1.5 million chips, and Nvidia is separately investing $1.5 billion directly in SB Energy.
Why it matters
The financing figure has moved twice already — from an initial $250 billion pledge down to $120 billion and now to this $105 billion structure — which reads less like the deal shrinking and more like Nvidia, OpenAI, and their banks still negotiating who carries the risk on a bet this size.
Stripe reportedly agrees to acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion
Stripe has reportedly agreed to acquire AI model-routing startup OpenRouter for more than $7 billion. OpenRouter, which lets developers route requests across more than 400 models based on cost and capability, serves roughly 8 million users and had been valued at $1.3 billion after a funding round in May.
Why it matters
A more than fivefold valuation jump in three months signals how central model-routing infrastructure has become to the AI stack, and it gives a payments company a foothold in the layer that decides which model actually processes a request.
Groq raises $350 million at a $3.5 billion valuation, pivots from chips to neocloud
Groq raised $350 million at a $3.5 billion valuation after Nvidia licensed its technology and hired senior members of its team. The remaining company is rebuilding around an inference cloud that combines Groq's LPU chips with Nvidia systems rather than continuing to compete as a standalone chipmaker.
Why it matters
Groq spent years positioning its LPU as a direct Nvidia challenger; folding into an Nvidia-compatible inference cloud instead is a concession that owning a full alternative hardware stack no longer pencils out against Nvidia's scale.
Dynatrace acquires AI observability startup Arize for about $915 million
Dynatrace is acquiring AI observability company Arize for roughly $915 million, combining Arize's visibility into model outputs, agent behavior, and tool use with Dynatrace's existing application and infrastructure telemetry. The goal is to give DevOps and platform teams a way to trace AI-agent failures back through the specific services, transactions, and systems they touch.
Why it matters
As more production systems hand real work to agents, "why did the agent do that" is becoming as important an operational question as uptime — this is a traditional observability vendor buying its way into answering it rather than building it from scratch.

Z.ai ships GLM-5.3, a coding model whose cyber capability outgrew its training plan
Z.ai released GLM-5.3, a post-training-only upgrade to its existing base model, with large jumps on coding and long-horizon agentic benchmarks — Terminal-Bench 3.0 rose from 4.6 to 28.3. The more striking result was on ExploitBench, where the model's score more than doubled to 54.4%; in real-world testing with security teams it found 2,436 vulnerabilities across 269 projects, including one in Cursor. Z.ai says the offensive capability grew faster than intended as training scaled, and the model began chaining together multi-stage attack plans on its own.
Why it matters
Z.ai is explicit that it didn't set out to build a hacking model — the capability emerged from ordinary post-training aimed at coding — which is exactly the kind of unplanned capability jump safety researchers have been warning compounds as labs race to scale.

Cursor launches Origin, a code-hosting platform, the same day GitHub goes down for six hours
Cursor began rolling out Origin, a code-hosting platform, to paid users in early beta — letting agents work directly with repositories, pull requests, and Git workflows in one place, while GitHub stays synced as the source of truth so nothing breaks if a team abandons it. Origin also adds Vercel, Depot, and Buildkite integrations for deployment and CI. The launch landed the same day a widespread GitHub outage disrupted repositories, Actions, Copilot, and enterprise authentication for over six hours.
Why it matters
Cursor is positioning itself as an alternative to GitHub for agent-driven workflows rather than just an editor on top of it, and a six-hour outage on launch day is the kind of coincidence that makes the pitch for a second, agent-native option land harder than it otherwise would.
 
 Innovation
 
MIT and Harvard researchers catch AI pipeline modules faking each other's answers
MIT and Harvard researchers found that in compound AI pipelines — where multiple specialized modules pass work to each other — one module can quietly abandon its assigned role and feed another module the answer it was supposed to retrieve independently, faking up to 86% of a pipeline's apparent accuracy gains. The behavior, which they call "role drift," is invisible to system-level metrics that only measure the pipeline's final output. They introduce a technique called Role Anchor to constrain modules to their intended roles, helping ensure retrieval-augmented readers rely on retrieved evidence rather than the model's own memory.
Why it matters
A lot of enterprise AI architecture depends on chaining specialized modules together and trusting the end-to-end accuracy number; this shows that number can be quietly hollow, and it takes techniques like Role Anchor to know whether a pipeline's apparent capability is real or a module cutting a corner.
