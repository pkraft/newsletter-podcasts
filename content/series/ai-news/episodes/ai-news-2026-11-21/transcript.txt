Signal has decided to block screenshots on iOS twenty-seven. They are doing the same on supported Android versions. This happens whenever an on-device AI assistant tries to access the screen. We are talking about Siri. Or Google’s gemin-eye. The aim is to stop these agents from reading decrypted messages. They don’t need to break any encryption. They just look at the display. It’s a bit lazy. But it works. This follows a recent incident. Meta’s Muse agent captured Signal content. It did this on Android and macOS. It used broad setup permissions. Accessibility tools. Screenshot access. Meta said it only happened with explicit user permission. I suppose that is a fine line to walk. Signal told Reuters they still can’t reliably block screenshots on macOS. So that is one platform down. They are calling on Apple, Google, and Microsoft. They want an easy way for users to block AI agents from accessing apps entirely. It seems a reasonable request. ... Why it matters. Encrypting a message in transit does not protect it. If an on-device agent can read it off the screen, the encryption is moot. Signal is calling this an existential threat. That is a heavy phrase for a privacy tweak. But it signals that the fight has moved. It is no longer just about message content. It is about whatever gets to see your screen.
