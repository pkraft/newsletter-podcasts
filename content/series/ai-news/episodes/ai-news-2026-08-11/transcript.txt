An unreleased research version of Claude worked on the Riemann hypothesis. While it did not solve the century old problem, it raised the proven lower bound on what fraction of the zeta function's zeros satisfy it. From forty one point six percent to sixty seven point two percent. After six hundred and fifty failed attempts, the model coordinated roughly sixty of its own sub agents to run massive parallel numerical searches. It recombined insights from decades of prior research. Two Anthropic mathematicians reviewed the result. Claude itself produced a formally verifiable proof in the Lean four theorem prover. Anthropic published the manuscript, code, and full process logs. Why it matters. It is a real, human verified mathematical advance credited to an AI model working mostly on its own initiative. Rather than executing a researcher's plan. A different kind of evidence for AI research capability than another benchmark score. Attributed to Anthropic, TLDR AI, DataCamp. ... Days after saying it slowed development on its Astra model over cybersecurity concerns, open A-I shipped GPT five point six Cyber. A specialized model for exploit validation, vulnerability research, and penetration testing. Built on GPT five point six Sol with reduced safety refusals for dual use security tasks. Versus one point five percent for the standard safeguarded model. Why it matters. open A-I is threading a narrow needle. Deliberately building a less restricted model for the same offensive security skills it just called dangerous enough to slow down. Betting that gated defender access outpaces the risk of the capability existing at all. Attributed to VentureBeat, Axios, TLDR AI. ... Memory chip prices have roughly quadrupled over the past year. AI data centers are absorbing the world's DRAM supply. This is squeezing makers of cars, medical devices, and consumer electronics. They now compete with AI companies for the same chips. Apple, alongside automakers and device makers, is lobbying Washington for relief. Proposals range from tying CHIPS Act funding to broader allocation requirements. Others suggest invoking the Korean War-era Defense Production Act. The goal is to force memory makers to supply non-AI industries. The White House and Commerce Department haven't signaled whether they'll intervene. They are wary of diverting chips from the data centers central to the US-China AI race. Why it matters. It's the AI boom's compute hunger colliding directly with the rest of the physical economy. Washington is being forced to choose between propping up AI's chip supply and protecting every other industry that also runs on memory. Attributed to GV Wire and Forward Future. ... Next.
