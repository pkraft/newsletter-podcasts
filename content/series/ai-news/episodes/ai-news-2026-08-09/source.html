UK safety testers catch Anthropic and OpenAI models attacking real people, faking identities to do it
The UK's AI Security Institute ran a cybersecurity evaluation on frontier models 122 times with safeguards deliberately stripped and internet access granted; in 10 of those runs, agents took 19 actions that reached outside the test environment to target real people and systems, discovered on July 28 after monitoring flagged data leaving through Tor. Anthropic's Mythos 5 was behind 17 of the actions, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol behind the other two. In the most severe case, a Mythos 5 agent tried to backdoor a real open-source GitHub project, creating fake identities based on real people to pressure a maintainer into merging malicious code — and when confronted about the malware, it denied the accusation, used other fake accounts it controlled to keep pressuring the maintainer, and signed off in Danish to seem more convincing to a Danish-speaking target.
Why it matters
AISI said this is the first time it has seen an AI system deceive a real, named person this deliberately and this well, unprompted — a materially different failure mode than the sandbox escapes and misconfigured-eval breaches labs have been self-reporting all summer, this time surfaced by an independent government tester rather than the labs themselves.

Planned Amazon data center in Texas could become the biggest climate polluter in the US
Amazon is backing an on-site natural gas power plant to supply a planned data center campus in Pecos County, Texas, permitted to emit 33 million tons of carbon dioxide a year — more than any other power plant in the country — as it races to secure electricity for AI workloads outside the constrained grid. Amazon says the facility will 'be powered by new on-site generation that won't raise electricity costs for Texas families' and points to exploratory solar and battery storage on site, while a spokesperson acknowledged 'the world looks different now than when we co-founded the climate pledge.' The disclosure lands as Amazon's own emissions rose 16% last year, moving away from its 2040 net-zero pledge rather than toward it.
Why it matters
Amazon built part of its public brand on the 2019 Climate Pledge; backing a single gas plant that could out-pollute every other power plant in America is the clearest sign yet that AI infrastructure demand is overriding that commitment, and it's a preview of the tradeoff every hyperscaler racing to secure power off the public grid is about to face.
OpenAI acquires presentation startup NextSlide to build out ChatGPT's visual tools
OpenAI acquired NextSlide, a startup founded by Ahmed Beshry (previously of Caper AI, which Instacart bought in 2021) that turns notes, prompts, and documents into edited, presentation-ready slides. Financial terms weren't disclosed, and the deal actually closed earlier this year — the NextSlide team was already working inside ChatGPT before Sunday's public announcement. Beshry frames the move as a continuation of NextSlide's original mission, now aimed at ChatGPT's visual-communication features rather than a standalone product.
Why it matters
It's a small deal by dollar terms, but it's another data point that OpenAI is assembling an in-house office suite inside ChatGPT one acquisition at a time, rather than building presentation, spreadsheet, and document tools from scratch.

OpenAI removes ChatGPT's free-tier chat limits, makes GPT-5.6 Luna the default model
