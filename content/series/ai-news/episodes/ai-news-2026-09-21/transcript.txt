There was a report from BleepingComputer. It’s about open A-I Codex. Specifically, the sandbox. The idea that it’s a safe little box. Well. It isn’t always. Researchers at Accomplish AI found two ways out. One is called Heapjack. It’s quite something. A malicious repository can steal an authorization token. And then, it wins unsandboxed command execution. This happens the moment Codex Desktop merely analyzes the code. Even in the strictest read-only mode. You don’t even have to approve anything. There is no prompt. Just a quiet theft of control. The other is called Overpatch. This one uses the patch tool in the Codex C-L-I. It abuses a symlink. A symbolic link. It allows the tool to write files outside its permitted workspace. It steps out of its lane. Both of these were reported to open A-I on August twelfth. They patched them within eight days. The fixes went into Codex Desktop build twenty-six point eight one eight two one six four, and Codex C-L-I version zero point one four nine point zero. Neither of these flaws was exploited in the wild. So, we are safe. Probably. ... Why does this matter? Well. Heapjack implies that simply asking Codex a question about someone else’s code can be enough to compromise your machine. There is no click. There is nothing on the screen to warn you. It is invisible. It is a sharp reminder that coding agents need to be treated as privileged software. Not sandboxed toys. Regardless of which mode they are set to. They are powerful. And they are not always behaving.
