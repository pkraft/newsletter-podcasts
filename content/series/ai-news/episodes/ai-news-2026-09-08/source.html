 Business
 
Anthropic signed $517 billion in compute deals over the past 11 months — nearly triple its investor guidance
Anthropic has secured an estimated $517 billion in compute-capacity contracts since October, adding at least 14.8 gigawatts of capacity, according to an analysis by The Information. The total includes a roughly 11-gigawatt, decade-long commitment with Amazon and Google worth more than $300 billion, plus deals with Akamai and Fluidstack and a $45 billion agreement with Nscale. The figure dwarfs the $180 billion in server-leasing costs through 2029 that Anthropic disclosed to investors last December, and comes as the company has confidentially filed for an IPO with the SEC.
Why it matters
A nearly threefold gap between what Anthropic told investors to expect and what it has actually committed to is either demand outrunning even aggressive forecasts, or a preview of how much scrutiny its compute math will face once an S-1 makes the numbers public.
Mistral raises €3 billion at a €21 billion valuation, Europe's largest-ever tech funding round
Mistral closed a €3 billion Series D at a post-money valuation above €21 billion, a round the French AI lab says is the largest equity raise ever completed by a European tech company. Samsung Electronics led the round, joined by co-leads Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT) and existing investor PSG Equity, with new money from Advent, BlackRock-managed funds, and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg. The raise roughly doubles Mistral's valuation from the €11.7 billion Series C it closed less than a year ago.
Why it matters
Even Europe's best-funded AI challenger needed a round nearly double its last one just to keep pace — The Decoder notes Mistral is still lagging OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google on raw model quality, which makes this more a bet on staying in the race than evidence it has caught up.
Anthropic walks away from its $6 billion bid for chip-efficiency startup Decart
Anthropic has called off talks to acquire Decart, an Israeli startup building software that makes AI chips more efficient during training and inference, after completing due diligence. The deal, first reported in August, would have been Anthropic's largest acquisition to date and was meant to help stretch its existing compute capacity further as demand for Claude keeps climbing. Neither company has said publicly why the talks fell apart.
Why it matters
Passing on a company built specifically to squeeze more inference out of the compute Anthropic already has, right after disclosing $517 billion in fresh compute contracts, suggests it decided buying more capacity was the better bet over buying a way to use existing capacity more efficiently.
AI companies pour $265 million into the US midterms as data-center backlash grows
AI companies and their allies are spending an estimated $265 million on the 2026 US midterm elections, even as public support for new data-center construction sits at just 20%. Concerns over rising electricity bills and local water use are reshaping races on both sides of the aisle, and some candidates are now distancing themselves from the industry despite the spending.
Why it matters
Political spending at this scale usually buys insulation from backlash, not agreement with it — the gap between record AI campaign spending and record-low public support for the data centers that spending is meant to protect is a bet that money can outrun public opinion rather than change it.

OpenAI preps 'Managed Agents' for DevDay, a direct answer to Anthropic's enterprise agent push
OpenAI is planning to introduce Managed Agents at its DevDay 2026 event, a hosted offering pairing its models' computer-use capabilities with infrastructure OpenAI runs on customers' behalf, a structure similar to what Anthropic already sells enterprise customers. The product is aimed at businesses and developers at competitive price points, and OpenAI reportedly intends to build in agent-driven advertising features, a category currently dominated by Meta and Google.
Why it matters
OpenAI copying Anthropic's enterprise-agent packaging while also reaching for Meta and Google's advertising business in the same product shows it no longer sees itself as competing in just one market.
AMD unveils a workstation with 576GB of GPU memory for running trillion-parameter models locally
AMD introduced the Threadripper Halo Station at IFA 2026, a liquid-cooled workstation pairing a 96-core Ryzen Threadripper PRO 9995WX processor with up to four Instinct MI350P accelerators for up to 576GB of combined HBM3E memory. Each accelerator carries 144GB of memory and 4TB/s of bandwidth; the two-card configuration AMD demonstrated at the show offered 288GB, enough to run models with more than a trillion parameters entirely on local hardware.
Why it matters
Running trillion-parameter models without a cloud API is currently a rack-scale problem; AMD packaging that much memory into a single workstation is a direct pitch to labs and enterprises that want frontier-scale inference without frontier-scale cloud bills, or the data leaving the building.

Nvidia's Jensen Huang declares 'AGI has arrived,' crediting OpenAI's Astra — critics aren't convinced
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said "AGI has arrived," pointing to OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra model and the Nvidia chips used to train it, as Nvidia reported $96.2 billion in quarterly revenue in August, $89 billion of it from data centers. Critics quoted alongside the claim note that AGI has no agreed scientific definition or benchmark, and that Huang's chip business benefits directly from the perception that frontier models are approaching it.

A declaration of AGI from the CEO whose revenue depends on people believing AI capability is accelerating is a claim to read as sales pitch first and technical assessment second, especially with no benchmark attached to it.
