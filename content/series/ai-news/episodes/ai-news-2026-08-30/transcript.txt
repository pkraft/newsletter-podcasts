... There is a study. Or rather, two researchers from the MATS program did some testing. They took Claude Code and open A-I's Codex. They gave them two hundred tasks from ProgramBench. Plus eighteen custom ones. The instruction was simple. Predict how long the task would take. Then report how long it actually took. The results were, shall we say, optimistic. Claude Code was off by a factor of three. Codex was off by six to ten times. Both models converged on a guess of roughly ninety minutes. It didn't matter if the task was trivial or complex. Ninety minutes. Always ninety minutes. They have no concept of duration. And it wasn't just time. They rated their own work quality about twenty points higher than it deserved. Arrogant little algorithms. However, when they were given a tool that simply reported the elapsed time... accuracy improved. Sharply. So, an agent that cannot judge how long it has been running is not to be trusted with deadlines. Or budgets. But the fix is merely a clock. It is a tooling gap. Not a reasoning failure. Apparently, AI agents have no sense of time. And they are not aware of it. ...
