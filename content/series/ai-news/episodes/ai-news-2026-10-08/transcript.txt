So. Finland. The licensing and supervisory authority there has ordered a stop. To work. At two planned data centers. In Muhos and Kajaani. Google had a subsidiary on the ground. They cleared forest. Roughly three hundred and thirty hectares at one site. Two hundred hectares at the other. But they hadn’t finished the required environmental impact assessment. So the regulator said, stop. No tree felling. No excavation. No road construction. All of it was tied to a thirteen billion euro investment Google announced for Finland just last month. Google has responded. Right. So, there is this thing called the Virtual Biology Initiative. It’s getting a bit of a boost. The US Department of Energy, the NIH, and a group called Biohub have announced an expansion. Google deep mind is involved. So is its drug-discovery spinout, Isomorphic Labs. And Meta. They’ve all committed three hundred million dollars in new funding. That brings the total commitment to one point eight billion dollars. The aim is to generate open datasets. Specifically, data on how cells respond to drugs, genetic edits, and other changes. This is the raw material for predictive "virtual cell" models. Now, if you are a commercial funder, you get a one-year embargo on the data you help pay for. After that, it becomes public. Why does this matter? Well, pooling funding across rival labs and the federal government to build shared biology data, rather than each lab hoarding its own, is a bet. It is a bet that virtual-cell modeling only gets good enough to matter if the underlying data is bigger than any one company could gather alone. ...
