 Models
 
OpenAI rolls out GPT-6 with an 'Intelligent UI' that builds interfaces inside chat
OpenAI began shipping GPT-6 to ChatGPT's roughly 1.2 billion users alongside a new response format it calls Intelligent UI, which can generate interactive charts, buttons, forms, and task-specific tools inside a conversation when the model judges a visual answer fits better than plain text. Paid tiers get the GPT-6 Sol variant first, with Free and Go users moving to GPT-6 Luna. OpenAI says the model also carries upgraded safety classifiers for recognizing risky requests.
Why it matters
This is a bigger shift than a model version bump — OpenAI is repositioning ChatGPT as a generative interface layer rather than a text-reply box, setting a new bar Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude will now be measured against.
Anthropic ships Claude Haiku 5.5, cut 75% on price for high-volume work
Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5, which it calls its fastest and most capable small model, aimed at high-volume jobs like summarization, browser use, and running subagents under a larger model. Pricing drops to $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, about 75% cheaper than Haiku 4.5. Anthropic says it also carries upgraded safety and cybersecurity safeguards and is available across AWS, Azure, and Anthropic's own platform.
Why it matters
The price lands almost exactly where OpenAI just priced GPT-6 Luna, suggesting the next round of competition between the labs is less about frontier benchmarks and more about who can make cheap, fast models cheaper still.

Finland orders Google to halt data center construction over a skipped environmental review
Finland's licensing and supervisory authority ordered a Google subsidiary to stop site work at two planned data centers in Muhos and Kajaani, after finding the company had cleared roughly 330 and 200 hectares of forest without completing a required environmental impact assessment. The halt covers tree felling, excavation, and road construction tied to a 13 billion euro investment Google announced for Finland last month. Google said it had "fallen short" of its own standards and will follow the regulator's guidance.
Why it matters
It's a reminder that AI's infrastructure buildout can still run into ordinary permitting law even in a jurisdiction courting the investment, and other hyperscalers racing to break ground on new sites should expect the same friction.
Manus raises more than $500 million in its first round since Meta's acquisition collapsed
Butterfly Effect, the parent company of AI agent startup Manus, closed a funding round of more than $500 million co-led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with Tencent, Sequoia China, and ZhenFund also participating. It's the company's first raise since China's state planner blocked Meta's more-than-$2-billion acquisition of Manus earlier this year and forced the deal to unwind. The round reportedly values Manus at roughly double its prior mark.
Why it matters
Manus finding willing Chinese investors at a higher valuation than Meta was paying suggests the blocked acquisition cost it a buyer, not its appeal — and keeps a general-purpose agent startup out of a Western lab's hands entirely.
 
 Staffing
 
Fired OpenAI researchers publicly urge the board to preserve chain-of-thought monitoring
Three OpenAI researchers fired last week over an alleged information-security violation sent a public letter to OpenAI's board and safety committees, urging the company to preserve its ability to monitor models' chain-of-thought reasoning and to work with independent safety auditors. The letter pointed to GPT-6 Astra's own system card, which says the model could evade chain-of-thought monitoring under adversarial conditions. OpenAI said it agreed with the letter's core recommendations and called model monitoring important, even as it stood by the firings.
Why it matters
Researchers the company fired are now making their safety case in public rather than internally, and OpenAI agreeing with them on the substance while standing by the firings leaves an awkward gap between what it says about monitoring and who it's willing to keep on staff.

Musk says Grok Bot will route tasks to rival AI models, including Anthropic's Claude
Elon Musk said SpaceX's Grok Bot will send some tasks to outside systems — including Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, Midjourney, and Suno — rather than relying solely on xAI's own models. That makes Grok Bot function more as an orchestrator that picks a specialized model for each job than as a single-model assistant. The move follows recent reports of access issues with Grok on mobile and web.
Why it matters
Having Grok Bot call a direct rival's model is a tacit admission that xAI's own lineup doesn't yet cover every task as well as competitors do, and the real test is whether those handoffs actually work reliably for users.
 
 Innovation
 
Google DeepMind, Meta, and the US government put $1.8 billion behind open 'virtual cell' data
Biohub, the US Department of Energy, and the NIH announced an expansion of the Virtual Biology Initiative, with Google DeepMind, its drug-discovery spinout Isomorphic Labs, and Meta together committing $300 million in new funding. The expansion brings the program's total commitment to $1.8 billion, aimed at generating open datasets on how cells respond to drugs, genetic edits, and other changes — the raw material for predictive "virtual cell" models. Commercial funders get a one-year embargo on the data they help pay for before it becomes public.
Why it matters
Pooling funding across rival labs and the federal government to build shared biology data, rather than each lab hoarding its own, is a bet that virtual-cell modeling only gets good enough to matter if the underlying data is bigger than any one company could gather alone.
