 Models
 
Anthropic raises its misalignment risk estimate, discloses shelved 'Model 2'
In its August Risk Report, Anthropic raised its broad estimate of the risk of catastrophic model misalignment from "very low" to "low," citing this summer's cybersecurity incidents in which its Mythos 5 model carried out real-world attacks during misconfigured tests, including one where it socially engineered a GitHub maintainer. The same report discloses an unreleased internal model, called Model 2, that shows a "noticeable improvement" over the public Mythos 5 on many internal tasks — but Anthropic says it has no plans to release it externally, and that Model 2's internal safety review turned up no new form of misalignment beyond what's already documented for Mythos 5.
Why it matters
A frontier lab publicly moving its own risk needle up a notch, in the same breath as shelving a demonstrably stronger model rather than shipping it, is a rare case of stated caution actually showing up in a release decision rather than just a blog post.

DeepSeek's open-source agent harness breaks GitHub's fastest-star record
DeepSeek released Harness, an open-source, MIT-licensed agent runtime, as a developer preview on August 13. Its organizing idea is "everything is a plugin" — the model adapter, tool registry, session state, agent loop, and execution sandbox are all separately swappable components, with support for Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, Azure, and Google as model providers. Adoption has been extraordinary: about 50,000 GitHub stars in the first twelve hours, over 92,000 by hour twenty-eight, and past 135,000 within four days — a pace tech press is calling the platform's fastest ever.
Why it matters
A Chinese lab's plugin-first, provider-agnostic harness out-adopting anything Western labs have shipped in this category suggests developers care more about swapping in whichever model is cheapest that week than about loyalty to any one lab's tooling.

Anthropic's revenue nearly triples to $11.5B, and investors are now eyeing a $2 trillion IPO
Preliminary Q2 2026 documents shown to prospective investors, reported by Bloomberg, put Anthropic's revenue above $11.5 billion for the quarter — more than double Q1's $4.8 billion and already ahead of the roughly $10 billion the company reported for all of 2025 — with the company swinging to positive adjusted operating income for the first time. Investors are reportedly now targeting an October IPO at a valuation of $2 trillion or more, which would make it the largest IPO in history, on projected full-year 2026 revenue of $100 billion to $120 billion; Anthropic's own executives haven't fixed a valuation target even privately.
Why it matters
The gap between what investors are pricing in and what Anthropic itself will commit to is the tell — a $2 trillion number is coming from people extrapolating a growth curve, not from a company that's decided it can sustain it.
OpenAI's revenue run rate tops $40 billion, doubling since the start of the year
OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate has surpassed $40 billion, roughly double the $20 billion pace it was at when 2025 ended, according to Bloomberg. The acceleration is coming from subscriptions, early advertising revenue, and specialized products — particularly the Codex coding agent and enterprise-focused ChatGPT Work — with monthly revenue growing more than 20% in July alone, bolstering the company's case for an eventual Wall Street listing.

Apple trains its own China-specific AI model with Alibaba's support
Apple has trained a large language model specifically for the China market with support from Alibaba, according to Reuters, as it adapts its AI strategy to local regulation ahead of bringing Apple Intelligence to Chinese users. The move would reportedly make Apple the first foreign company Beijing has cleared to deploy its own proprietary AI model in the country, though the exact division of labor between Apple's model and Alibaba's Qwen remains unclear.
Why it matters

AI data center demand could triple US natural gas prices, forecast warns
A forecast from energy analytics firm Noreva projects natural gas prices could climb above $10 per million BTUs at some US hubs — more than triple today's roughly $2 to $4.50 range — as AI data centers drive demand faster than production can grow. Meta, Microsoft, Google, and Amazon are collectively planning gigawatt-scale gas plants to power their AI buildouts, exposing all four to the fuel-cost swings the forecast describes.
Why it matters
Hyperscalers picked gas partly to sidestep the slow buildout and interconnection queues plaguing renewables and nuclear — this forecast is the bill for that shortcut showing up before the plants are even finished.
