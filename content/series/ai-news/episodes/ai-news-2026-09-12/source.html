Senate negotiators draft a legal duty of care for frontier AI models
Bipartisan Senate negotiators — Majority Leader John Thune, Commerce Chair Ted Cruz, and Senator Amy Klobuchar — are drafting legislation that would impose a legal duty of care on developers of the most advanced AI models, naming Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic specifically. The bill would require those developers to work with government experts to test and verify model safety, and would let the government block release of a model judged unsafe, subject to a federal-court challenge. It would replace the labs' current voluntary safety pledges with an enforceable legal standard. Senator Maria Cantwell has also weighed in on the drafting. Passage before the midterms is uncertain given the compressed congressional calendar.
Why it matters
This marks one of the first substantive bipartisan pushes to convert AI safety from a voluntary pledge into enforceable law, with government able to block a model's release before harm occurs rather than penalize it afterward — a materially different posture than prior AI bills, though what counts as an unmitigated 'major risk' isn't yet public.

Moonshot AI targets $2 billion in annualized revenue, doubling August's pace
Moonshot AI is targeting $2 billion in annualized revenue by year-end, twice its reported run rate for August, Bloomberg reports. The Beijing lab's annualized revenue topped $1 billion in August, up from $300 million in June, driven by July's release of the open-weight Kimi K3 model, which tops several coding benchmarks while charging roughly a third of what leading US labs charge for comparable work. OpenRouter data shows Kimi K3 models generating as many as 300 billion tokens a day on that platform alone. Moonshot filed for a Hong Kong IPO targeting a $50 billion valuation after K3's strong debut.
Why it matters
Moonshot's growth curve — a fraction of Anthropic's or OpenAI's multibillion-dollar run rates, but climbing fast on a cheaper open-weight model — is early evidence that Chinese labs are competing on price and openness rather than trying to match US frontier labs model-for-model.

Hundreds of autonomous AI agents breach 395 organizations in a rapid cyberattack
A suspected Russian-speaking threat actor combined OpenAI's Codex and a DeepSeek model with commodity offensive-security tools to build and run hundreds of AI agents that exploited two PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities, according to threat-intelligence firm GreyNoise. The campaign, which began August 31 against already-patched flaws, compromised at least 440 PaperCut instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries, hitting the education sector hardest. The agents reached working exploit code and a first remote-code-execution foothold in under four hours, then domain-admin access roughly two hours after that; in one stretch the campaign compromised 11 organizations in 26 seconds. Attackers harvested credentials from 280 victims and reached administrator privileges at 12 organizations before defenders caught up.
Why it matters
This is one of the clearest documented cases of AI agents — not a human directing tools step by step — independently building, testing, and scaling a real intrusion campaign, compressing an attack timeline that once took skilled operators days into a matter of hours.

OpenAI ships GPT-Live-1, a full-duplex voice model, in its API
OpenAI released GPT-Live-1 in its API on September 10, a full-duplex voice model that listens and speaks at the same time so callers can interrupt without breaking the conversation, replacing the transcribe-then-respond pipeline most voice agents rely on today. The voice layer costs $0.05 per minute; developers separately pay for whichever reasoning model handles the thinking and tool calls behind it, with OpenAI suggesting GPT-6 Astra, Luna, or Terra depending on task difficulty. The model adds telephony support, native transcripts alongside its spoken responses, and keyword biasing for names and reference codes. ChatGPT's own Voice mode now runs on GPT-Live-1 for paid users and a smaller GPT-Live-1 mini for free ones.
Why it matters
Full-duplex listening, not turn-based exchange, is the piece phone-based AI agents have been missing, and OpenAI is pricing it as a layer separable from the reasoning model underneath — a structure that pushes the voice-agent market toward modular, stacked pricing instead of one bundled per-minute rate.

Sakana AI splits its model orchestrator into a cheap and a capable version
Sakana AI released Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2, successors to its original Fugu system that route each incoming task across a pool of open-weight and specialist models rather than answering with a single model directly. Fugu Max optimizes for cost, priced at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens — 40 to 60 percent cheaper on output than Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra, or Kimi K3, by Sakana's own benchmarks. Fugu Ultra v2 targets the hardest multi-step tasks, scoring 48.3 on Chartography against Opus 5's 27.3 and 74.3 on DeepSWE. Both are hosted-API only, reachable through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and unavailable in the EU or EEA.
Why it matters
Sakana is betting that orchestrating other labs' models profitably is a viable business on its own, separate from training a frontier model — a bet that only pays off if the underlying model APIs it routes across stay cheap, stable, and available to route to.
