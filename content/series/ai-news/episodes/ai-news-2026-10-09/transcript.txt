... Anthropic has launched a service called OSS Scanner. It is free. And it is optional. You have to opt in. It uses their Claude and Mythos models to scan open-source projects for security bugs. It does this repeatedly. And it reports the findings. It does not wait for a human reviewer to say yes first. It just finds the holes and tells you. In a six-month pilot, the scanner found over twenty-nine thousand candidate vulnerabilities. That is a lot of holes. But the team could only manually check about six thousand of them. Of those, eighty-eight percent of the high-severity ones were considered valid enough to disclose. If you maintain a foundational open-source project, especially one tied to critical infrastructure, you can apply. You just need to submit a pull request to the project's GitHub repository. The real story here is the gap. Twenty-nine thousand candidates. Six thousand checked. That gap is the headline. Even with a frontier model doing the scanning, human review is still the bottleneck. That is the bottleneck this tool has to prove it can shrink. Not just move it somewhere else. But actually make it smaller. ...

open A-I has released over seven hundred manuscripts. They are all AI-generated mathematical research. They consulted with outside mathematicians.
