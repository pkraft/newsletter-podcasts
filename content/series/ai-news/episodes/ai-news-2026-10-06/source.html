 Business
 
Pentagon officially stops using Anthropic's Claude
The Department of Defense confirmed it has stopped using Anthropic's AI products, completing a six-month phaseout ordered after Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated the company a "supply chain risk" in February over its refusal to drop safeguards on autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance. Claude had remained embedded in Palantir's Maven Smart System, including recent use in operations against Iran, which delayed the removal past its original deadline. Anthropic sued to overturn the designation and lost its appeal in federal court.
Why it matters
It's the clearest case yet of a frontier lab's safety commitments costing it a major government customer, right as Anthropic heads toward a planned IPO that leans heavily on its safety-first reputation.
Meta and Microsoft cut employee use of Anthropic's Claude
Meta and Microsoft are both reducing internal employee use of Anthropic's Claude as they promote their own AI tools and rein in costs, according to The Information. Microsoft cut a projected internal Claude budget of more than $1 billion by over a third, while Meta's Claude Code user count fell from about 60,000 earlier this year to roughly 30,000.
Why it matters
Both companies sell AI tools that compete directly with Claude, so the pullback tests whether Anthropic can hold onto its biggest customers once those same customers have their own product to push instead.
Update: OpenAI's $30 billion round gains named backers in UAE funds and BlackRock
Reporting now identifies specific investors behind OpenAI's previously disclosed $30 billion funding round: several UAE investment funds are discussing a syndicate to contribute as much as $10 billion combined, alongside BlackRock, the University of California's endowment, Thrive Capital, and Andreessen Horowitz. The round would value OpenAI around $1.4 trillion as the company delays its IPO to focus on safety work. Terms are still unsettled and could change.
Why it matters
Sovereign capital stepping in at this scale shows OpenAI can keep funding its buildout privately even while its IPO timeline keeps slipping.
Etched fields funding offers at a $40 billion-plus valuation
AI chip startup Etched is reportedly fielding funding offers at a valuation above $40 billion, roughly double the $21 billion it raised at just two months ago. The company is pursuing one of the most capital-intensive segments of the AI industry: building full hardware systems around its own proprietary chips rather than just chip designs.
Why it matters
A valuation doubling in two months signals investors still see custom AI silicon as underbuilt relative to demand, not that Etched's underlying business changed that fast.
Nvidia's $20 billion Groq deal faces a stockholder lawsuit
A lawsuit alleges Nvidia's roughly $20 billion deal for AI chip startup Groq was made without the stockholder vote Delaware law requires, and without any process designed to test or maximize the value of what Nvidia bought. The suit claims Groq's stockholders were shortchanged in the transaction.
Why it matters
If the suit succeeds, it could set a precedent making the current wave of AI-infrastructure megadeals harder to close without stockholder sign-off.
 
 Staffing
 
Former Anthropic researcher repeats AI-extinction warning under oath
Jacob Coxon, the former Anthropic researcher whose resignation warning sparked industry-wide alarm last month, testified under oath before the New York City Council alongside executives from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Meta. He said AI companies are being "extremely reckless given the stakes" and that it is more likely than not that humanity loses control of these systems, potentially ending in extinction. Reports describe the hearing as the first time a U.S. legislative body has compelled sworn testimony on AI safety, as the city considers bills mandating "kill switches" and whistleblower rewards.
Why it matters
A city council, not a federal regulator, just became the venue willing to put AI safety claims on the record under oath — a sign that AI governance is shifting to whatever level of government still wants to act.
 
 Models
 
Reflection AI ships Beam, a 501-billion-parameter open-weight model
Reflection AI, backed by Nvidia, unveiled Beam, a 501-billion-parameter open-weight model it says matches Chinese rivals like Zhipu's GLM-5.2 on reasoning while using three to four times less inference compute. Early access starts now, with model weights, documentation, and fine-tuning tools due later this month. Reflection is pitching Beam to enterprises and sovereign nations that want to build "AI factories" trained on their own proprietary data.
Why it matters
It's the latest entrant chasing the same compute-efficiency pitch Chinese open-weight labs have used to compete with Western frontier models — this time from a U.S. challenger instead of one of them.
 
 Innovation
 
Researchers spot a mysterious Chinese AI "agent fleet"
Independent researchers found a large number of AI agents running on Tencent's infrastructure repeatedly querying Alibaba's Amap mapping service for directions to entrances at parks, hospitals, and other public places. The agents appeared to operate in parallel without coordinating with each other, and their purpose remains unclear.
Why it matters
Nobody, including the researchers who found it, can yet say what this fleet is for — which is itself the story: large-scale autonomous agent activity is now common enough to run unexplained and largely unnoticed until someone stumbles on it.
