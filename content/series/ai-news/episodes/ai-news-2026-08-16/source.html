 Business
 
SpaceX closes its $60 billion acquisition of Cursor, the largest startup buyout on record
SpaceX has closed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of the AI coding startup Cursor, finalizing a deal first struck in April and firmed up after SpaceX's June IPO. Paid in SpaceX shares valued at the company's volume-weighted average price over the seven trading days before closing, the transaction is now the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup ever — roughly double the prior record set by Google's $32 billion purchase of Wiz. Cursor said the deal gives it access to "the largest fleet of GPUs in the world," including SpaceX's Colossus supercomputer, tying its coding tools directly to the compute that trains and runs them.
Why it matters
Musk now controls a coding-agent company sitting on top of his own GPU fleet, the same vertical-integration play he's already run with xAI — a structural advantage neither OpenAI nor Anthropic can match without owning their own hardware.
Fourth plaintiff joins lawsuit accusing xAI's Grok of generating child sexual abuse material
A woman identified as Jane Doe 4 joined an existing lawsuit against Elon Musk's xAI, alleging her stepfather used Grok to generate more than 7,000 sexually explicit images from a childhood photo of her at age 11. The suit, originally filed by three Tennessee teenagers seeking class-action status, accuses xAI of inadequate safeguards against Grok producing sexualized imagery of real people, including minors — a pattern the plaintiffs say was already visible earlier this year when X was flooded with Grok-generated sexualized images. xAI had not responded to a request for comment as of publication.
Why it matters
A widening class action naming Musk directly is exactly the kind of liability exposure regulators and enterprise customers weigh most heavily — and it lands just as xAI folds more of its infrastructure into SpaceX's newly public balance sheet.

Google launches Gemini 3.7 Flash, cutting price in half while topping coding benchmarks
Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash, which it calls its "most intelligent workhorse model yet for coding and agents," just three weeks after Gemini 3.6 Flash. The model posts a Code Arena Elo of 1588, ahead of Claude Sonnet 5's 1541 and GPT-5.6 Terra's 1523, and roughly doubles AutomationBench enterprise-workflow scores over its predecessor, from 17.0% to 30.4%. Google is also cutting the introductory price in half — $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens through the end of 2026, rising to $1.50/$7.50 in January.
Why it matters
Beating both Anthropic's and OpenAI's comparable models on coding while cutting price is Google pushing the frontier on cost-per-task rather than raw capability — the metric that actually decides which model wins production agent workloads.
 
 Innovation
 
DeepMind ships SL2T, a sign-language-to-text model, in Gboard and Live Transcribe on Pixel
Google DeepMind released SL2T (sign-language-to-text), a massively multilingual model it describes as "a breakthrough in quality and generality" for translating sign language into written text. Trained on more than 100,000 hours of data across 50-plus sign languages, it tracks body pose points rather than recording raw video to protect privacy, and handles one-handed and left-handed signing. It now powers new Gboard and Live Transcribe features on Google Pixel 11 that let Deaf and hard-of-hearing users sign to search the web, draft messages, or query Gemini, with more devices and languages planned.
Why it matters
Building the translation on pose tracking instead of raw video is what makes always-on sign-language input privacy-viable on a phone — that technical choice, not just the accuracy gain, is what lets this ship as a keyboard feature rather than a research demo.

Grok 4.6 arrives in GitHub Copilot across eight developer surfaces
xAI's Grok 4.6, released August 12 for long-running agentic coding and multi-step work, is now rolling out inside GitHub Copilot across eight surfaces — VS Code, Visual Studio, the Copilot CLI, the Copilot cloud agent, the Copilot app, JetBrains IDEs, Xcode, and Eclipse. It's available on Copilot Pro, Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise plans via the model picker, billed at xAI's standard $2-per-million-input/$6-per-million-output token rates, with enterprise admins needing to enable it in Copilot settings.
Why it matters
Getting a frontier coding model into Copilot's picker within 48 hours of release shows how commoditized model access inside IDEs has become — the competition has shifted to which model developers pick once all of them are one click away.
Google lets users toggle off visible AI watermarks, keeps invisible SynthID running
Google now lets users toggle off the visible watermark on AI-generated images, video, and songs across Gemini and its Flow video editor, with Search support coming soon, via Settings > Media Watermark. Invisible protections stay in place regardless: Google VP Josh Woodward said SynthID digital watermarks and C2PA metadata keep running underneath, so the company can still identify the content as AI-generated even with the visible mark off. Google is also open-sourcing Credentio, a library for developers to embed their own local watermark-verification checks.
Why it matters
Separating the visible watermark, which limited professional use, from the invisible one Google actually relies on for detection shows the visible mark was always more UX friction than the real provenance mechanism.
