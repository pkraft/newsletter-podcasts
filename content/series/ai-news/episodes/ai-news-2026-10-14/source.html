OpenAI and Anthropic offer UK regulators a conditional 'kill switch'; DeepMind and Meta hold back
Testifying before a UK parliamentary committee, OpenAI and Anthropic said they would halt a model if the UK's AI Security Institute or an intelligence agency flagged it as unsafe, and both companies said at least some AI safety rules should be legally binding. Google DeepMind and Meta agreed on the need for external scrutiny and testing but stopped short of endorsing new legislation. Separately, DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis said this year's agent-escape and misalignment incidents are 'early warning shots' from systems that 'aren't that powerful yet,' and that the industry has roughly two years to build better safeguards before one does real damage.
Why it matters
The labs furthest ahead of regulators are usually the ones writing the rules; here OpenAI and Anthropic are volunteering for binding oversight before Westminster can force it, while DeepMind's own CEO is the one putting a two-year clock on the industry's safety margin.
Anthropic nears a $6.3 billion Osaka data center deal with NTT Data
Anthropic is reportedly in final talks with NTT Data to build its first Japan-based data center, a site in Osaka that could draw up to 500 megawatts of power and cost as much as $6.3 billion. The two sides are aiming to sign by the end of Japan's current fiscal year, with part of the facility operating as early as 2028. Nothing is signed yet, and neither company has confirmed the plan.
Why it matters
Anthropic's compute deals so far have clustered in the US and UK (Nscale, Broadcom, SpaceX); a company-dedicated site in Japan signals the search for power and land has gone global, and Osaka's grid will face the same scrutiny that's already slowed data-center buildouts in Finland and across the US this month.
FBI arrests an OpenAI employee accused of planting hidden recording devices in its offices
OpenAI said it alerted the FBI after finding a hidden recording device, with advanced encryption and audio-monitoring capability, hidden inside a USB port on the back of a TV in an executive conference room at its San Francisco office. The FBI arrested the employee, Abdulrahman Alfozan, a member of OpenAI's product team since July 2025, and charged him with illegally intercepting oral communications. OpenAI said it found no evidence he accessed or exfiltrated model weights, source code, or customer data, though it believes he planned to plant additional devices elsewhere in the building.
Why it matters
A listening device built to evade detection and planted by an insider on the product team, not an external attacker, is a reminder that OpenAI's real security exposure this year has run through its own badge-holders about as often as through anything a model has done.
The New York Times, AFP, and The Economist join the SPUR publisher coalition on AI content use
The SPUR coalition, founded in February by the BBC, Financial Times, Guardian, Telegraph, and Sky News to set shared standards for how generative AI systems use publisher content, added more than 40 new members including The New York Times, The Economist, and Agence France-Presse. SPUR now counts over 80 members across 20-plus countries. The coalition doesn't license content on members' behalf; it writes shared technical and commercial standards, including a recently released Content Telemetry Standard, so publishers can track how AI platforms use their work and negotiate their own compensation.
Why it matters
Publishers suing AI companies one at a time has produced years of litigation and few settlements; a shared measurement standard that over 80 outlets can point to turns 'you used our content without paying' from an unprovable claim into an auditable one.

Nvidia open-sources its Run:ai GPU orchestration platform
Nvidia released the full Run:ai platform as open-source software: a Kubernetes-native system for GPU scheduling and AI workload orchestration, with fractional GPU allocation, gang scheduling, and team-level quotas that let multiple teams share one cluster. The release splits into four independently adoptable pieces, including the KAI Scheduler, now a CNCF Sandbox project under Apache 2.0. Eight DSX ecosystem partners, including Crusoe, Nebius, and Rafay, are already integrating the open-source version.
Why it matters
Nvidia makes far more money renting out GPUs than licensing scheduling software, so giving away the orchestration layer that decides how efficiently those GPUs get used is a bet that commoditizing the software stack sells more chips than it cannibalizes.
Physical Intelligence opens its Pi API beta, renting out robot foundation models
Physical Intelligence opened a waitlisted beta for the Pi API, letting outside developers upload robot data, fine-tune the company's π0 and π0.5 generalist robotic foundation models on infrastructure Physical Intelligence manages, and run inference through a hosted gateway. The company says it can update the models underpinning the API quickly and plans to keep rolling in its newest, most capable checkpoints. An early partner, Almond, says its dual-arm Axol robot learned to deburr spacers used in its own assembly after four days of iterating against the API.
Why it matters
This turns a frontier robotics lab into a hosted-inference business the way OpenAI and Anthropic already are for language models, and it bets that robot foundation models improve fast enough that renting access beats a customer trying to keep up by self-hosting.

Liquid AI ships LFM2.5-24B-A2B, an open-weight reasoning model built to run on-device
Liquid AI released LFM2.5-24B-A2B, an open-weight mixture-of-experts model with 24 billion total parameters but only about 2 billion active per token, aimed at agents running locally on laptops and phones rather than in the cloud. Compared with its predecessor, the context window grew from 32,000 to 131,000 tokens and the vocabulary doubled to better handle non-Latin languages, and the model now produces an explicit chain of thought before answering. Liquid AI says it leads or ties on 5 of 8 agentic and tool-use benchmarks against the larger Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, while running up to 1.7x faster on-device.
Why it matters
A 24-billion-parameter model that only activates 2 billion of them per token is Liquid's bet that on-device agents need to be judged on tokens-per-second and battery draw, not just benchmark scores, and that frontier-adjacent capability can ship small enough to run without a cloud bill.
